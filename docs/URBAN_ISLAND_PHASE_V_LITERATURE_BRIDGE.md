# Literature bridge: from urban/island fragmentation mechanisms to Phase-V state sufficiency

## Scope

Urban and island systems are not ecological equivalents. They are useful because they provide contrasting causal routes through the same candidate condition space. The prospective question is whether different fragmentation mechanisms can converge on the same operational interaction-mediated functional-fragmentation regime.

Phase V does not simulate cities or islands. It tests a necessary mathematical condition for that comparison: whether layer-wise state marginals are sufficient, or whether cross-layer spatial alignment must also be retained.

## Literature-derived causal decomposition

### Landscape structure is not one fragmentation scalar

Functional-fragmentation research in landscape ecology emphasizes organism-centred connectivity rather than geometry alone (Benitez et al. 2025). A large 2026 landscape experiment further separates habitat loss, configuration and surrounding matrix quality. Therefore habitat amount, spatial configuration and matrix permeability should remain distinct empirical inputs rather than one patch-count axis.

### Interaction structure and function are not reducible to species richness

Pollinator functional diversity can predict trait matching and pollination function when species richness does not (Hiraiwa & Ushimaru 2024). Multi-habitat landscapes can exhibit interaction complementarity, evenness, robustness and pollination performance not obtained by simply summing component habitat webs (Hackett et al. 2024). Empirical network structure predicts temporal persistence (Dominguez-Garcia et al. 2024), and later work shows rewiring among persistent partners can increase year-to-year persistence even when much interaction turnover is driven by species turnover (Dominguez-Garcia et al. 2026). Recent synthesis treats both topology and interaction-strength rewiring as resilience mechanisms (Ward et al. 2026).

These findings motivate retaining the relation between where interaction support occurs and where functional/trait support occurs, rather than only their separate totals.

### Urban route: interaction turnover can decouple from gene flow

Urban plant-pollinator interaction composition can vary more with season and local urbanization intensity than with spatial distance; interaction rewiring and plant turnover are major components (Marcacci et al. 2023). A 2026 review of urban plant population genetics reports that urban populations often retain genetic diversity comparable with non-urban populations and that gene flow can remain high through long-distance and anthropogenic dispersal (Hardion et al. 2026). Thus a city can retain genetic connectivity while local interaction support is reorganised.

### Island route: interaction filters, turnover and reproductive assurance can dominate

Oceanic-island pollination networks are often smaller and lower in interaction diversity than mainland networks, but island area/isolation do not explain every network property (Traveset et al. 2016). Canary Island multilayer networks show that regional species turnover and local interaction structure both matter, with species turnover dominating some regional distance-decay patterns (Vitali et al. 2024). Global island work identifies mutualist association as a candidate biogeographic filter beyond classic area/isolation effects (Delavaux et al. 2024), although this result has a published statistical critique and author response and should not be treated as an uncontested universal law. Reproductive-assurance theory likewise predicts selection on the capacity for uniparental reproduction under colonisation or mate limitation rather than universal high selfing.

### Genetic state is not a universal proxy for ecological function

A plant meta-analysis found small positive ecological associations for adaptive/genotypic diversity but no overall relationship for neutral molecular diversity (Whitlock 2014). A global fine-scale genetic-structure meta-analysis found no consistent overall fragmentation/degradation effect and strong heterogeneity associated with dispersal biology (Miguel-Penaloza et al. 2023). Peled et al. (2026) establishes that heterogeneous landscape connectivity can generate genetic early-warning signals before rapid genetic transitions; the present study instead targets interaction-dependent functional loss and asks whether that event process is sufficiently defined before warning is evaluated.

## Why cross-layer alignment is the missing state variable candidate

The literature implies that the same layer-wise totals can be assembled differently in space:

- urban sites may retain pollen/seed/human-mediated genetic exchange while interaction partners turn over locally;
- islands may have strong geographic isolation but variable mutualist availability, stepping-stone movement and reproductive assurance;
- functional diversity and rewiring can buffer or destabilise function independently of species richness;
- matrix quality can change effective connectivity without changing habitat amount.

Therefore a mechanism-agnostic regime defined only by separate means or marginal distributions of habitat support, interaction state, genetic state and functional-trait support may erase biologically important information.

Phase V tests the minimal counterexample: two states with identical layer-wise marginals and genetic-diversity summaries but opposite spatial alignment between interaction support and the genetic/trait-support bundle. If their transition or loss process differs, the regime definition must include cross-layer alignment or an equivalent sufficient joint-state statistic.

## Empirical translation if Phase V rejects coarse-state sufficiency

The urban/island comparison should estimate a joint state rather than independent indices. At minimum retain:

1. habitat amount/configuration and matrix quality;
2. patch-specific realised interaction strength and partner identity;
3. functional diversity, contribution evenness, turnover and rewiring;
4. patch-specific realised functional endpoint;
5. pollen, seed/propagule, demographic and partner movement as separate processes;
6. reproductive assurance or alternative functional routes;
7. neutral and, where available, adaptive/functional genetic state;
8. cross-patch covariance/alignment among these layers.

Convergence would then mean different causal routes arrive at a joint state with comparable downstream functional-loss dynamics. It would not require similar geography, species composition, network topology or neutral genetic differentiation.
