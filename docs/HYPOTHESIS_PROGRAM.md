# Condition-first hypothesis program

## Central hypothesis

> **Spatial fragmentation becomes interaction-mediated functional fragmentation only under eco-genetic conditions that weaken or destabilise an interaction-supported functional state. Genetic warning is interpretable only after the distribution of functional loss is characterised well enough to support a finite validation design.**

The repository follows one rule: if a proposition is not general, recover its **condition or boundary** rather than widening the search until a desired warning result appears.

Phase J adds an important design correction: the historical R4 rule is an **all-block finite-panel calibration certificate**, not by itself a sample-size-invariant biological regime. The primary biological object is now the distribution of functional-loss events across stochastic blocks.

```text
C0  FUNCTION EXISTS
    interaction-dependent high-function state
        ↓
C1  FUNCTION IS VULNERABLE
    fragmentation / deterioration can disrupt it
        ↓
C2  FUNCTIONAL-LOSS DISTRIBUTION IS CONDITIONED
    recurrent turnover + connectivity + interaction / partner processes
        ↓
    central incidence + among-block heterogeneity / tails
        ↓
    finite-panel calibration certificate (legacy R4 gate)
        ↓
C3  WARNING IS CONDITIONAL
    only after an independently characterised loss process
        ↓
C4  PORTABILITY IS BOUNDED
    warning need not transfer across eco-genetic domains
```

## C0–C1 — inherited mechanism

The pinned parent repository (`eco-genetic-criticality`, scientific commit `dd8ee379d0d3518194c767d16402042525bc00dc`) supplies the bounded mechanism:

- the declared positive-feedback interaction map can support a high-function state;
- high interaction can support a high-investment trait mode under the declared trait-margin condition;
- projecting the same prepared state into equal isolated fragments lowers interaction, local effective size and realised high-trait mass;
- population persistence, realised function, allele state and genetic diversity remain distinct.

**C0: recovered for the declared model closure.**
**C1: recovered as bounded parent theory plus finite Type S fragmentation evidence.**

## C2 — conditions shaping the functional-loss distribution

All C2 campaigns are warning-blind: genetic-diversity decline, warning time, lead/lag ordering and lead time are unavailable during source/loss analysis.

### C2a — recurrent turnover changes source feasibility

Across the original 15 recurrent-transition coordinates, **2,269/3,375** source attempts supported preparation/projection. Coordinate support ranged from **44.89% to 86.67%**.

**Status: supported, finite Type S.**

### C2b — recurrent turnover changes functional-loss outcomes

Among 648 complete common-grid candidates:

- 322 rapid-loss;
- 242 persistence;
- 84 seed-heterogeneous;
- 0 strict R4 certificates.

The historical 15/15 `no_domain_selected` result remains immutable for that coarse candidate family. Prospective warning-blind refinement later recovered an R4 certificate twice at `kappa_mu=0.35, p_star=0.35`; immediate neighbouring `p_star` values were R3 in those finite panels.

**Post-Phase-J correction:** these results show that recurrent turnover changes source feasibility and the distribution of loss histories, and that a finite warning-validation panel can be found. They do **not** establish a sample-size-invariant biological “R4 region.” Phase J proves that the all-block R4 certificate is ensemble/design sensitive when tail blocks have nonzero probability.

**Status: supported distributional effect plus bounded finite-panel certificate.**

### C2c — genetic-state connectivity reshuffles loss histories; the categorical `m=0.10` boundary is not portable

Phase E, at one independently generated five-seed ensemble, produced:

| migration rate | pooled loss | finite-panel certificate |
|---:|---:|---|
| 0.000 | 0.571 | R4-highrep |
| 0.025 | 0.549 | R4-highrep |
| 0.050 | 0.593 | R4-highrep |
| 0.100 | 0.626 | R3-highrep |
| 0.200 | 0.604 | R3-highrep |

Paired trajectories switched loss status in both directions at every nonzero level. This is the robust Phase-E result: connectivity changes **which stochastic histories lose function** without a universal rescue/collapse sign.

Phase I later generated an independent fresh ensemble at the same nominal `m=0.10` condition and classified it R4. Phase J then held `m=0.10` fixed across 20 new seed blocks and obtained three R4 five-seed panels and one R3 panel. Therefore the Phase-E `m=0.10` R3 label remains a valid locked finite result but is **not a portable categorical connectivity boundary**.

`migration_rate` is allele-frequency mixing only, not demographic, pollen, seed, pollinator or recolonisation movement.

**Status: bidirectional trajectory effect supported; exact categorical `m=0.10` boundary downgraded as ensemble-sensitive.**

### C2d — aggregate interaction support did not alter the finite certificate across the tested range

Phase F used only the original Protocol 002 interaction-feedback values `kappa = 3.0, 4.5, 6.0`, with fresh independent source reconstruction at every kappa.

| interaction kappa | source / baseline eligible | pooled loss | finite-panel certificate |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 3.0 | 77/100 | 0.468 | R4-highrep |
| 4.5 | 94/100 | 0.521 | R4-highrep |
| 6.0 | 87/100 | 0.552 | R4-highrep |

All 15 seed-block rates in that campaign were inside `[0.30,0.70]`. The bounded result remains useful: source feasibility varied while the finite-panel certificate did not change over the predeclared scalar-support range.

`interaction kappa` is aggregate feedback strength, **not partner richness**, connectance, pollinator diversity or network dimensionality. Phase F is closed; no finer/wider kappa search is opened.

**Status: bounded negative scalar-support result, finite Type S.**

### C2e — matched partner loss changed block heterogeneity more than mean risk

Phase G used a fresh intact four-partner control and three prospectively fixed one-partner-loss architectures matched for `4→3` richness change and mean retained support `0.75`.

| partner condition | pooled loss | seed-rate range | finite-panel certificate |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| intact control | 0.544 | 0.129 | R4-highrep |
| even redundant loss | 0.567 | 0.261 | R3-highrep |
| graded-contribution loss | 0.556 | 0.353 | R3-highrep |
| dominant-partner loss | 0.578 | 0.235 | R3-highrep |

Paired loss status switched in both directions. A labelled secondary paired-incidence audit gave Cochran Q `p=0.943`; contribution concentration itself did not separate the three loss conditions.

After Phase J, the strongest interpretation is distributional rather than categorical: the tested partner-loss perturbation broadened among-block outcomes enough to fail the conservative all-block certificate in that ensemble while mean incidence remained similar. The Phase-G R4→R3 labels should not be promoted to a universal partner-loss boundary.

**Status: supported mean-risk/reproducibility separation; finite certificate change retained as design-specific evidence.**

### C2f — explicit rewiring recovered network structure but not the downstream loss certificate

Phase H prospectively represented six candidate partners, four initially active edges, two latent edges, explicit edge strengths, trait-match scores, capacities, balanced primary-partner loss and one fixed dynamic rewiring rule.

The opening rule succeeded: fresh intact = R4; matched partner loss/no rewiring = R3. The constrained rewiring condition remained R3 (`not_rescued`).

Network recovery was substantial:

| diagnostic | no rewiring | constrained rewiring |
|---|---:|---:|
| final active edges | 3 | 5 |
| realised connectance | 0.500 | 0.833 |
| latent edges activated | 0 | 2 |
| final support multiplier | 0.750 | 0.844 |

Pooled loss changed only `0.430→0.419`; rewiring altered only one of 86 paired loss statuses.

The robust conclusion is therefore not that rewiring is universally ineffective. It is:

> **network structural recovery and match-weighted interaction-support recovery are not sufficient evidence that the downstream functional-loss distribution has recovered.**

This result survives the Phase-J gate correction because it compares explicit network diagnostics with realised loss outcomes directly.

**Status: bounded negative rewiring result; network-state/function-distribution decoupling supported.**

### C2g — pollen movement can be separated mechanistically from generic allele mixing

Phase I introduced paternal pollen-mediated gene flow. Under the declared diploid random-mating closure,

`p_off = (1-g/2)p_local + (g/2)p_pool`.

For a census-weighted regional pollen pool this is exactly legacy global allele mixing at `m=g/2`. At `g=0.20`, **90/90** completed regional-pollen trajectories were snapshot-exact with paired legacy `m=0.10` trajectories.

At the same `g=0.20`, regional and ring-neighbour pollen kernels both produced R4 finite-panel certificates, while paired loss status switched bidirectionally (7 loss→no loss; 8 no loss→loss for regional→ring).

Thus spatial pollen kernel changed individual stochastic histories without changing the finite certificate in that campaign. The exact `g↔m/2` identity is narrow to this regional paternal-gene-flow closure and does not make `migration_rate` a generic pollen-dispersal parameter.

**Status: exact operator bridge plus bounded negative spatial-kernel certificate result. Phase I closed.**

### C2h — the historical all-block R4 gate is ensemble- and panel-size dependent

Phase J fixed the complete `m=0.10` biological condition and generated 20 fresh master-seed blocks. The four prospectively fixed five-seed panels classified:

- Panel 1: R4;
- Panel 2: R3;
- Panel 3: R4;
- Panel 4: R4.

The preregistered outcome was **`ensemble_sensitive`**.

Across all 20 blocks, **19/20** rates lay inside `[0.30,0.70]`; the only failure was `12/16 = 0.75`. Mean block loss was `0.525`, median `0.513`.

The design dependence is exact. If a random block passes the band with probability `q`, an independent `B`-block all-pass certificate succeeds with probability

`q^B`.

For every `0<q<1`, certificate probability declines mechanically with panel size. In the observed Phase-J set, all `C(20,5)=15,504` five-block combinations partition exactly into:

- `11,628` R4 panels = **75%**;
- `3,876` R3 panels = **25%**.

Therefore **R4 is now interpreted as a finite-panel calibration certificate under a specified design, not a sample-size-invariant biological regime**.

The primary biological estimands are the loss distribution itself:

1. central event incidence;
2. among-block heterogeneity and tail behaviour;
3. optionally, the block-level probability/fraction of falling inside an operational incidence zone, reported with uncertainty.

The legacy all-block certificate remains useful as a conservative opening rule for a specific warning-validation panel, but it is downstream of those distributional estimands.

**Status: ensemble sensitivity recovered; exact classifier design boundary Type T; Phase J closed.**

## C3 — genetic warning only after the loss distribution is characterised

The inherited symmetric benchmark remains a bounded proof of possibility: in one independently calibrated domain, baseline-relative `H_alpha`/`H_gamma` decline preceded 35 observed functional losses at all six tested relative endpoints. Fixed absolute thresholds produced both leads and lags.

Phase J changes the interpretation of the upstream gate, not the historical warning trajectories. Future warning validation should characterise event incidence and among-block heterogeneity first, then use any finite R4-like certificate only as an explicit sampling-design rule.

A matched recurrent-transition-direction warning effect remains unresolved; no direction-only warning campaign is opened by post-result tuning.

**C3: conditional possibility recovered; universal threshold and direction-only causal effects unresolved.**

## C4 — warning portability is bounded

Historical Protocol 003 recovered two warning-evaluable finite domains by separate warning-blind calibration. They differ in recurrent-transition, ecological and deterioration parameters. Their warning differences remain **bounded portability evidence across calibrated eco-genetic domains**, not an isolated mutation-direction effect.

Phase J adds a further caution: portability of a binary calibration label should itself be checked across independent ensembles rather than assumed.

**C4: bounded non-portability result; causal attribution intentionally limited.**

## Exact theory boundaries

`RECURRENT_TRANSITION_DIVERSITY_THEORY.md` remains authoritative for the recurrent-transition Type T identities. `classification_gate_theory.py` adds the sampling-design identity for the historical all-block gate.

Type T statements now include:

- recurrent-transition direction has no universal signed effect on heterozygosity;
- transition strength contracts among-patch allele-frequency differences independently of direction under fixed weights;
- stronger local high-state support can coincide with lower heterozygosity;
- allele-frequency homogenisation is not a theorem of functional rescue;
- for an all-block certificate with block-pass probability `q` and panel size `B`, certificate-pass probability is `q^B`, so the certificate is not panel-size invariant unless `q∈{0,1}`.

## Urban and island application

Urban and island landscapes remain contrasting empirical routes through the same condition space, **not ecological equivalents and not demonstrated here to converge**.

Field translation should separate:

- structural fragmentation, habitat amount and matrix quality;
- realised interaction support and partner identity;
- functional diversity, contribution evenness and rewiring;
- pollen, seed/propagule, demographic and partner movement;
- reproductive assurance / alternative functional routes;
- realised function through time;
- the distribution and reproducibility of functional loss across comparable populations/years;
- genetic state through time.

Phase H sharpens the empirical rule: recovery of connectance or interaction support alone does not establish recovery of the functional-loss process. Phase I shows why biological movement should be identified by process and spatial kernel rather than inferred from a generic connectivity parameter. Phase J shows that a city/island unit should not be assigned a hard “warning-evaluable regime” from a small all-pass panel without uncertainty analysis.

The prospective question remains:

> **Do different fragmentation mechanisms converge on the same operational interaction-mediated functional-fragmentation distribution once state feasibility, realised function, process-resolved connectivity and among-unit loss heterogeneity are measured separately?**

## Global stop rules

Do not:

1. inspect warning/diversity fields while selecting or describing upstream loss conditions;
2. refine `p_star`, connectivity, interaction kappa, partner weights, rewiring strength, pollen fraction or the historical R4 gate merely to obtain a desired categorical result;
3. overwrite any locked finite campaign because a later independent ensemble classifies differently;
4. call allele-frequency migration demographic/pollinator/seed rescue;
5. call interaction `kappa` network simplification;
6. call Phase G a full network experiment or Phase H a universal rewiring theorem;
7. call the Phase-E `m=.10` R3 result a portable categorical boundary;
8. call R4 a sample-size-invariant biological regime after Phase J;
9. open a direction-only warning test without prospectively matched conditions;
10. treat failed generality or ensemble sensitivity as unfinished tuning rather than a scientific result.
