# Condition-first hypothesis program

## Central hypothesis

> **Spatial fragmentation becomes interaction-mediated functional fragmentation when it weakens the process maintaining realised ecological function. Genetic warning is interpretable only after the downstream loss process has been characterised independently in terms of state feasibility, loss incidence, block heterogeneity and trajectory identity.**

The repository follows one rule: if a proposition is not general, recover its **condition or boundary** rather than widening the search until a desired result appears.

```text
C0  FUNCTION EXISTS
    interaction-dependent high-function state
        ↓
C1  FUNCTION IS VULNERABLE
    fragmentation / deterioration can disrupt it
        ↓
C2  LOSS PROCESS IS CONDITIONED
    source feasibility + loss incidence + block heterogeneity + trajectory identity
        ↓
C3  WARNING IS CONDITIONAL
    genetic lead time is tested only after C2 is fixed warning-blind
        ↓
C4  PORTABILITY IS BOUNDED
    warning need not transfer across calibrated eco-genetic domains
```

## Historical screen versus biological estimands

The original warning-blind calibration classified five-block candidates as R1, R2, R3 or R4 using fixed block-rate boundaries at 0.30 and 0.70. Those labels remain immutable protocol facts. Phases J–L showed that the all-five-block screen is strongly finite-sample sensitive at historical block sizes and cannot identify biological heterogeneity by itself.

Final inference therefore separates:

- **loss incidence**;
- **between-block heterogeneity**;
- **trajectory-identity sensitivity**;
- **warning performance**.

R1–R4 remain historical screening labels and do not replace these estimands.

**C0: recovered for the declared model closure.**  
**C1: recovered as bounded parent theory plus finite Type S fragmentation evidence.**

## C2 — conditions generating functional loss

All C2 campaigns are warning-blind. Warning times, genetic lead/lag ordering and lead time are unavailable during condition selection or replication.

### C2a — recurrent turnover changes source feasibility

Across the original 15 recurrent-transition coordinates, **2,269/3,375** source attempts supported preparation/projection.

**Status: supported, finite Type S.**

### C2b — recurrent turnover primarily changes loss incidence along the tested frontier

The original common deterioration family contained 648 complete candidates: 322 historical rapid-loss, 242 persistence and 84 historical R3/mixed-block candidates. No candidate met the original strict R4 screen, so the historical 15/15 `no_domain_selected` result remains immutable for that family.

High-precision exact-seed replays recovered an incidence frontier:

| `p_star` | pooled loss | historical screen | equal-rate p |
|---:|---:|---|---:|
| .325 | .682 | outside R4 | .295 |
| .350 | .546 / .538 | R4 | .370 / .252 |
| .375 | .407 | R4 | .693 |
| .400 | .273 | outside R4 | .151 |

**Conclusion:** the former “narrow R4 bounded by seed-heterogeneous neighbours” interpretation is withdrawn. The supported pattern is a high-to-low **incidence frontier** with no detected excess block heterogeneity at these four tested coordinates.

### C2c — the historical allele-mixing heterogeneity did not freshly replicate

In the exact historical Phase-E/M seed family, high-precision pooled loss remained near .54–.56 across `m=0,.025,.05,.10,.20`. Only `m=.10` showed excess equal-rate heterogeneity (`p=.0205`); paired marginal-risk tests were null.

Phase U then preregistered one independent fresh five-seed replication at the same anchor with only `m=0` and `m=.10`, 100 attempted replicates per seed, at least 70 eligible trajectories per block, identical paired eligibility and no requirement that the control pass R4.

| ensemble / condition | pooled loss | screen | equal-rate p |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| historical Phase M `m=0` | .559 | R4 | .710 |
| historical Phase M `m=.10` | .557 | R3 | **.0205** |
| fresh Phase U `m=0` | .540 | R4 | .134 |
| fresh Phase U `m=.10` | .551 | R4 | **.745** |

Fresh paired McNemar `p=.694`. The preregistered decision is **`historical_m010_heterogeneity_not_freshly_replicated`**.

**Conclusion:** the Phase-M `m=.10` signal remains a valid observation in its historical seed family, but it is not supported as an independently reproducible parameter-specific heterogeneity effect. Phase U is one replication and does not prove that no future seed family could ever show heterogeneity.

**Status: historical seed-family observation + one preregistered fresh non-replication; no reproducible connectivity heterogeneity threshold recovered.**

### C2e — reduced-form and temporal partner perturbations are bounded negative results

Phase N high-precision pooled loss was .556/.544/.565/.549 for intact/even/graded/dominant partner conditions; all were block-homogeneous and paired marginal-risk tests were null despite trajectory switching.

Phase T held expected support at .75 while realised support variance increased `0 → .04684 → .09702`. Pooled loss was `.5442/.5488/.5533`; equal-rate and paired McNemar tests were non-significant.

**Conclusion:** the historical low-replicate partner-loss R4→R3 claim is withdrawn. Temporal support variance and contribution concentration did not produce a detected population-level loss-process effect under the declared closure. The preregistered adaptive-rewiring gate remains **closed**.

### C2f — process-resolved movement narrows the historical connectivity interpretation

Phases R/S used the historical Phase-M seed family and compared the historical allele-only `m=.10` observation with two more explicit movement closures.

| closure | pooled loss | historical screen | equal-rate p | paired marginal result |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| no connectivity | .559 | R4 | .710 | reference |
| historical-family allele-only `m=.10` | .557 | R3 | .0205 | null vs isolation |
| whole-individual dispersal `d=.10` | .606 | R4 | .811 | McNemar `p=.143` vs no connectivity |
| pollen-only paternal gene flow `g=.20` | .532 | R4 | .728 | McNemar `p=.311` vs no connectivity; `.266` vs allele-only |

**Conclusion:** within the historical Phase-M seed family, the heterogeneity pattern did not port to whole-individual or pollen-only movement. Combined with Phase U, the strongest supported statement is now negative: **no robust portable connectivity heterogeneity effect was established across the tested ensembles and operators.**

`migration_rate` remains allele-frequency mixing, not demographic, pollinator, pollen, seed or recolonisation movement.

## C3 — full-denominator warning validity after C2

The inherited symmetric benchmark established event-conditional warning ordering. In the frozen H2-R domain, baseline-relative `H_alpha` and `H_gamma` erosion at 5%, 10% and 20% preceded all **35/35** observed functional losses at each endpoint. Fixed absolute thresholds produced both leads and lags.

Phase V prospectively repeated exactly that relative-warning family in one independent fresh seed ensemble (`20291110–20291114`) without recalibrating the loss domain, changing the deterioration schedule or selecting warning endpoints after outcomes. Of 100 attempted trajectories, 82 were available and 33 reached realised functional loss. For **all six** endpoints, the same-trajectory ordering was:

- valid pairs: **33**;
- leads: **33**;
- ties: **0**;
- lags: **0**;
- exact one-sided binomial `p=1.1641532182693481e-10`.

The preregistered event-pair decision was **`strict_replication`**. The post-review audit then restored all baseline-eligible trajectories to the denominator. Each endpoint also fired in all 48 inherited and 49 fresh non-event trajectories. Full-horizon specificity was zero and binary-marker AUC was 0.5 in both ensembles.

> **Within the frozen symmetric H2-R state, relative-diversity crossings reproduced event-conditional temporal ordering but did not discriminate losses from non-events.**

The frozen protocol result remains provenance, but the predictive early-warning claim is withdrawn. No new continuous score, endpoint, threshold or favourable landmark is opened after this result.

Phases R/S/T/U add no warning claims; they constrain or replicate the upstream loss-generating closure. A direction-only causal warning effect remains unresolved, and no outcome-informed tuning is opened to obtain one.

**C3: event-conditional ordering replicated; predictive warning validity not supported.**

## C4 — warning portability

Historical Protocol 003 recovered two domains by separate warning-blind calibration. They differ in recurrent-transition, ecological and deterioration parameters. Their fresh-seed warning differences are therefore **bounded portability evidence across calibrated eco-genetic domains**, not a single-factor effect of transition direction.

After the full-denominator correction, C4 is supporting portability evidence rather than a positive warning headline. The stricter lesson is that event-only ordering can replicate inside a frozen state while predictive discrimination remains absent.

**C4: bounded non-portability result; causal attribution intentionally limited.**

## Urban, island and other natural applications

Urban and island systems are contrasting empirical routes through the condition space, not ecological equivalents. Natural measurement now follows a common sequence: define direct function, measure the synchronized process state, add upstream origin/history last, and test whether it improves held-out prediction.

Two direct open-data partial-state tests now implement that logic:

- Honshu–Izu E1: mainland distance did not improve leave-one-site-out pollen-function prediction after `TM_z + FDQ + FEve + season + focal plant`; aggregate MSE was about 4.08% worse after adding distance. The partial state itself remained only modestly predictive.
- Zurich E2: 0/6 fixed reproductive endpoints showed a preregistered positive held-out gain from `PlantS + Urban_500 + PlantS×Urban_500` after the source-defined interaction state.

Neither is a full eco-genetic convergence proof because synchronized `G/C/R/M` layers are incomplete. They are direct tests of whether habitat/context labels retain transferable information after measured process state.

### N1 — direct urban–island convergence is not identifiable from the existing archives

The stronger hypothesis is whether a common future-relevant state makes fragmentation origin redundant across systems:

`F_future ⟂ origin | S_measured`.

A prospective cross-origin identifiability gate compared the already locked Honshu–Izu and Zurich designs before fitting any pooled outcome model. The direct test stopped for four linked reasons:

1. **state semantics are not aligned:** Honshu–Izu uses `TM_z + FDQ + FEve`, whereas Zurich uses focal-function-specific effort-standardised pollinator-guild visitation vectors;
2. **responses are not aligned:** standardized pollen receipt is not prospectively interchangeable with the Zurich Poisson/binomial fruit and seed endpoints;
3. **origin is perfectly confounded with study/protocol identity:** there is only one archive per origin;
4. therefore a genuine whole-system cross-origin validation is not available.

Generic z-scoring is explicitly prohibited as a repair because numerical scaling does not establish biological equivalence of state coordinates. No pooled urban-versus-island outcome model was fitted.

**N1 status: `cross_origin_convergence_not_identifiable_from_existing_archives`.** This is a design/identifiability boundary, not evidence that urban and island systems differ.

The minimum identifiable future design is now fixed: the same biological state coordinates, the same direct realised-function endpoint, and origin replicated independently of study identity — at least two independent systems per origin or one coordinated multi-origin protocol — followed by whole-system/landscape transfer validation. The *Oenothera* counterexample also requires process-specific connectivity/mating opportunity to be retained when the endpoint depends on pollen or mate movement.

## Global stop rules

Do not:

1. inspect warning/diversity fields while selecting or replicating C2 conditions;
2. reinterpret historical R1–R4 labels as latent biological classes;
3. refine `p_star`, migration, interaction kappa, partner weights or the historical screen merely to obtain a desired result;
4. overwrite the historical 15/15 Protocol 002 no-domain result;
5. call allele-frequency mixing demographic, pollinator, pollen, seed or recolonisation movement;
6. describe `m=.10` heterogeneity as independently replicated or an established connectivity threshold;
7. rerun fresh Phase-U ensembles or replace seeds merely to recover significance;
8. call interaction `kappa` network simplification;
9. call the Phase-N/T partner layers a full dynamic multispecies network;
10. open adaptive rewiring after the Phase-T null result merely to rescue an effect;
11. open a direction-only warning test without prospectively matched conditions;
12. retain a mechanistic claim solely because a finite seed family crosses a significance or historical screen threshold;
13. rerun Phase V, replace its fresh seeds, alter the frozen domain or add warning endpoints merely to obtain a different replication classification;
14. infer cross-state warning portability from the within-state Phase-V replication;
15. pool urban and island archives by generic z-scoring of semantically different state variables or infer a cross-origin effect when origin equals study identity.
