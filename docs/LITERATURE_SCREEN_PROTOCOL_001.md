# Protocol 001 — initial literature screen

**Screen date:** 2026-07-02  
**Status:** initial direct-precedent screen; not yet a claim of exhaustive absence.

## Question screened

Has a prior study explicitly proposed and tested the following kind of dynamic
hypothesis?

> Under a direction-specific recurrent-mutation closure, does a relative decline
> in genetic diversity precede a realised trait-loss first-passage event?

The target is not the isolated presence of mutation bias, genetic diversity,
evolutionary rescue, or ecological early warnings. It is their **specific
time-ordering proposition** in a finite, stochastic eco-genetic model.

## Included nearest literatures

| literature | what it establishes | critical difference from H2-R-AS |
|---|---|---|
| Drake & Griffen (2010), *Early warning signals of extinction in deteriorating environments* | Ecological early-warning signals can precede extinction in deteriorating laboratory populations. | The warning is ecological critical slowing down, not a genetic-diversity first passage; there is no recurrent directional mutation or trait-loss event. |
| Orr & Unckless (2014), *The population genetics of evolutionary rescue* | Population-genetic theory links mutation, adaptation, and persistence/rescue. | The outcome is rescue/extinction probability, not whether a genetic-diversity warning precedes a realised trait-loss event. |
| Marrec & Bitbol (2020), *Adapt or perish: Evolutionary rescue in a gradually deteriorating environment* | Stochastic simulations connect mutation, deterioration rate, mutant appearance/fixation, and extinction/rescue time. | It does not compare relative H-alpha/H-gamma first passages with trait-loss first passage, and it does not use direction-specific recurrent mutation. |
| Ashander, Chevin & Baskett (2016), *Predicting evolutionary rescue via evolving plasticity in stochastic environments* | Quantitative-genetic variation, stochastic demography, and environmental predictability jointly affect persistence. | It does not formulate a genetic-diversity early-warning ordering or a directional recurrent-mutation contrast. |
| Alfaro & Ducrot (2018), *Population invasion with bistable dynamics and adaptive evolution: the evolutionary rescue* | Eco-evolutionary coupling and an asymmetric **gene-flow** term can alter persistence in bistable dynamics. | Asymmetry is dispersal/gene flow, not recurrent mutation; no diversity-warning versus trait-loss event-time test is made. |
| Svensson & Berger (2019), *The Role of Mutation Bias in Adaptive Evolution* | Mutation bias is a recognised modifier of adaptive evolution and must be separated from selection and standing variation. | It does not pose an ecological early-warning/trait-loss first-passage hypothesis. |
| Teixeira & Huber (2021), *Dismantling a dogma: the inflated significance of neutral genetic diversity in conservation genetics* | No simple universal association should be assumed between neutral diversity and extinction risk. | It is a warning against universal interpretation, not an event-time model; it strengthens the need for a closure-specific test rather than undermining it. |

## Direct-precedent result of the initial screen

No included study was identified that simultaneously contains all of the following:

1. **direction-specific recurrent mutation** rather than symmetric mutation,
   generic mutation supply, or asymmetric gene flow;
2. a **finite stochastic eco-genetic** process with explicit ecological feedback;
3. a **realised trait-loss first-passage event** distinct from extinction or
   persistence probability;
4. **relative genetic-diversity first passages** as warning events; and
5. a design that separates **trait-loss-only calibration** from fresh-seed
   warning validation while retaining censored outcomes.

This is a **no-direct-match-found** result, not proof that no direct precedent
exists. The systematic source-by-source screen required by
`NOVELTY_REVIEW_PROTOCOL.md` remains open.

## Current defensible positioning

Until the full screen is complete, Protocol 001 may be described as:

> A new, explicitly formulated closure-conditional hypothesis: directionally
> biased recurrent mutation may change whether relative genetic erosion precedes
> realised high-trait loss.

It must **not** yet be described as the first study to address that question.

## Consequence for model design

The literature screen supports retaining the following distinctions in code and
reporting:

- mutation direction is not gene flow asymmetry;
- realised trait loss is not synonymous with population extinction;
- relative genetic erosion is not assumed to be a universal extinction proxy;
- a successful SYM bridge is a control, while UP and DOWN are separate biological
  closures; and
- calibration must remain blind to warning values to avoid selecting a schedule
  because it produces an appealing lead.
