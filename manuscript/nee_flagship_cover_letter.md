# Cover letter — Nature Ecology & Evolution

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Article, **“Relational eco-genetic state governs functional vulnerability under fragmentation,”** for publication in *Nature Ecology & Evolution*.

Fragmentation ecology commonly tracks habitat geometry, abundance, interactions and genetic diversity as separate indicators of deterioration. Our study asks a more fundamental question: **what must an ecological state retain if it is to determine future function?** We answer this using three complementary mathematical boundaries and prospectively bounded finite-model tests.

First, an exact interaction-state result and a fresh fixed-area fragmentation gradient show that potential viability, realised occupancy, interaction support, effective size and realised trait mass do not collapse onto one deterioration axis. Potential high-trait viability changes from 1,037/1,037 supported one-patch outcomes to 0/1,037 at every tested subdivision, while realised occupancy persists at approximately 99.6–100% at generation 30.

Second, we formalize transition sufficiency. A coarse representation is sufficient only if the exact next transition factors through that representation. We construct two admissible eco-genetic states with identical census, interaction, allele-frequency and trait marginals, `H_alpha`, `H_gamma` and `F_ST`, yet opposite cross-layer spatial alignment. Their exact next interaction fields differ by as much as **0.2543**, proving that the retained marginals cannot determine the next state. A separately prospectively locked 1,500-pair experiment then shows that this hidden relational distinction propagates to approximately **+5.33** and **+5.20 percentage-point** functional-loss risk differences at generations 20 and 40 under the declared deterioration path.

Third, we show why an early marginal response need not identify ecological fate. Six frozen diversity thresholds precede every observed functional loss in two independently seeded ensembles (35/35 and 33/33) but also fire in every horizon non-event (48/48 and 49/49). Their specificity is therefore zero and binary-marker AUC is 0.5. An exact denominator identity shows that perfect event-conditioned precedence fixes sensitivity but leaves specificity mathematically unconstrained.

Together these results support a positive ecological conclusion: **functional vulnerability under fragmentation is relational rather than purely compositional.** Persistence, marginal diversity and even early erosion can all remain informative while failing to identify future function if the representation discards the organization through which ecological and genetic components jointly generate that function.

Published natural systems are used only to interpret this result, not to validate the finite model. Urban *Crepis* illustrates uncompensated local interaction limitation; Miyake-jima *Camellia–Zosterops* illustrates movement-mediated compensation; *Conospermum* illustrates cohort/history lag; and *Spondias* illustrates coordinated interaction–connectivity–function deterioration. These contrasts motivate the broader implication that `urban`, `island` and `fragmented` are upstream histories, not sufficient functional states by themselves.

The manuscript is prepared as a Nature Ecology & Evolution Article with four main display items. All load-bearing numerical results are generated from pinned, locked evidence objects. A separate natural-data programme is retained only as empirical projection and prospective measurement design; it is not presented as external validation or pooled evidence for the synthetic mechanism.
