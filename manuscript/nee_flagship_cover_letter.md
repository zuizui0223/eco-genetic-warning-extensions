# Cover letter — Nature Ecology & Evolution flagship

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Article, **“Ecological prediction requires validated state, signal and measurement,”** for publication in *Nature Ecology & Evolution*.

Ecological forecasting is often approached as a problem of model choice and uncertainty, but a forecast can fail earlier: the biological state may be conflated with a disturbance label, the analytical representation may erase future-relevant structure, an early signal may lack discrimination, or a natural proxy may not have earned endpoint relevance. We test these four failure points using complementary evidence rather than proposing one universal ecological state or warning statistic.

The Article combines four prospectively bounded evidence layers. First, a theorem-guided fragmentation programme shows that potential viability, realised occupancy, interaction support, effective size and realised trait mass do not respond as one deterioration state. Second, a constructive matched-summary experiment shows that states with identical common ecological and genetic marginals can differ in their exact next transition by 0.2543; a separately prospectively locked 1,500-pair experiment shows that the hidden state distinction propagates to an approximately five-percentage-point functional-loss risk contrast at generations 20 and 40 under the declared deterioration path. Third, six frozen diversity thresholds reproduce perfect event-conditioned temporal precedence in two independently seeded ensembles but also fire in every non-event, yielding specificity zero and binary AUC 0.5; an exact denominator result explains why perfect precedence cannot identify prospective discrimination. Fourth, seven locked natural-data analyses show distinct measurement, representation and identifiability boundaries rather than one pooled ecological effect.

The synthesis supports one general conclusion: **ecological predictability is a property of a validated chain from biological state, through target-relevant representation and full-denominator signal validation, to empirically adequate measurement—not of a familiar summary or early signal in isolation.** The four analyses are deliberately not joined by an unsupported causal story: fragmentation is not claimed to cause the matched-state construction, omitted alignment is not claimed to cause warning failure, and the natural datasets are not presented as external validation of the synthetic model. Their integration is inferential: each establishes a distinct non-implication that must be checked before the next predictive claim is licensed.

We believe this fits the broad ecology and evolution readership of *Nature Ecology & Evolution* because it addresses a general problem upstream of forecasting algorithms and connects ecological state, forecast horizon, early-warning validation and empirical indicator measurement in one testable hierarchy.
