# Cover letter — Nature Ecology & Evolution

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Article, **“Competing eco-genetic pathways govern functional vulnerability under fragmentation,”** for publication in *Nature Ecology & Evolution*.

Fragmentation ecology commonly tracks habitat geometry, abundance, interactions and genetic diversity as separate indicators of deterioration. Our study asks why systems retaining the same marginal ecological and genetic quantities can nevertheless move toward different functional outcomes. We combine exact state results with prospectively locked intervention experiments to identify the causal structure behind that divergence.

First, an exact interaction-state result and a fresh fixed-area fragmentation gradient show that potential viability, realised occupancy, interaction support, effective size and realised trait mass do not collapse onto one deterioration axis. Potential high-trait viability changes from 1,037/1,037 supported one-patch outcomes to 0/1,037 at every tested subdivision while realised occupancy persists at approximately 99.6–100% at generation 30.

Second, we formalize transition sufficiency and construct two states with identical census, interaction, allele-frequency and trait marginals, `H_alpha`, `H_gamma` and `F_ST`. Their exact next interaction fields differ by as much as **0.2543**. The immediate cause is analytically identifiable: cross-layer covariance changes spatial support variance **49-fold while mean support remains identical**.

We then prospectively decomposed the long-horizon mechanism rather than assuming that one alignment direction was protective. A preregistered trait-by-allele factorial showed strong non-additivity: trait–allele mismatched states had **6.23 percentage points** higher functional-loss risk than matched states at generation 20 and **4.70 points** higher risk at generation 40. In a second preregistered intervention, removing direct trait and allele contributions to the interaction-support signal made the initial interaction field identical between the aligned and reversed states, yet the reversed state still had **7.13** and **6.93 percentage-point** higher loss risk at generations 20 and 40. This demonstrates an indirect matching-dependent recruitment/selection pathway. By contrast, restoring direct eco-genetic feedback into interactions largely removes the directional aligned-versus-reversed difference, showing that the feedback itself can compensate spatial mismatch by strengthening weak local interaction states.

The mechanism is therefore not “alignment is good.” **Functional fate emerges from competition between matching-dependent recruitment and feedback-mediated compensation.** This also explains why a marginal variable can be biologically responsive without discriminating fate: six frozen diversity thresholds precede every observed loss in two independently seeded ensembles but also fire in every non-event, giving specificity zero and binary AUC 0.5.

Published natural systems are used only to interpret these mechanisms, not to validate the finite closure. Urban *Crepis* illustrates limited compensation; Miyake-jima *Camellia–Zosterops* provides a natural example of compensatory reorganization; *Conospermum* illustrates temporal mismatch; and *Spondias* illustrates coordinated deterioration. These examples motivate prospective tests of matching, compensation and memory across fragmentation contexts.
