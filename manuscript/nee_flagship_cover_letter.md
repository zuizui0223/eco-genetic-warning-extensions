# Cover letter — Nature Ecology & Evolution

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Article, **“Eco-genetic sorting and buffering shape functional vulnerability under fragmentation,”** for publication in *Nature Ecology & Evolution*.

Fragmentation ecology commonly tracks habitat geometry, abundance, interactions and genetic diversity as separate indicators of deterioration. Our study asks a more mechanistic question: **why can systems retaining the same marginal ecological and genetic quantities nevertheless reach different functional outcomes?** We combine exact state results with prospectively locked intervention experiments that progressively decompose the responsible life-cycle pathways.

First, an exact interaction-state result and a fresh fixed-area fragmentation gradient show that potential viability, realised occupancy, interaction support, effective size and realised trait mass do not collapse onto one deterioration axis. Potential high-trait viability changes from 1,037/1,037 supported one-patch outcomes to 0/1,037 at every tested subdivision while realised occupancy persists at approximately 99.6–100% at generation 30.

Second, we formalize transition sufficiency and construct states with identical census, interaction, allele-frequency and trait marginals, `H_alpha`, `H_gamma` and `F_ST`. Their exact next interaction fields differ by as much as **0.2543**. The immediate cause is analytically identifiable: at identical mean support, cross-layer covariance changes spatial support variance **49-fold**.

We then asked prospectively why such relational differences persist or disappear. A locked trait-by-allele factorial showed that mismatched states had **6.23 percentage points** higher functional-loss risk than matched states at generation 20 and **4.70 points** higher risk at generation 40, while the aligned-versus-reversed direction itself was not stable under full feedback. Removing direct trait and allele contributions to interaction support nevertheless exposed a reproducible indirect matched-state advantage. On fresh seeds the reversed state had **4.20** and **4.40 percentage-point** higher loss risk at generations 20 and 40.

A second prospectively locked edge-deletion experiment changed our mechanistic interpretation. Deleting allele-linked recruitment enlarged the reversed-state disadvantage to **13.20** and **12.73 percentage points**, identifying recruitment as a buffer of mismatch. Deleting local ecological selection as a joint block removed the late advantage: the preregistered generation-40 difference-in-differences was **+7.27 percentage points** (95% CI +2.67 to +11.87). Removing density from the interaction update eliminated all functional losses by generation 20, identifying density feedback as a gate for entry into the declared collapse regime.

We then isolated the leading single edge in a third, separately locked prospective experiment. The local high-allele selection operator can be written exactly as

`logit(p+) - logit(p) = log(0.75 + 0.4q)`,

so it is strictly increasing in local interaction state and switches direction at `q*=0.625`, the same threshold at which the declared high trait becomes potentially viable. The same operator strictly increases spatial covariance between q and high-allele log odds whenever q varies among patches.

The focused endpoint test used **6,000 paired AA/RR keys per condition** from twelve entirely new master seeds. With local q-dependent allele selection retained, generation-40 `RR-AA` functional-loss risk was **+6.65 percentage points** (95% CI +5.07 to +8.23). Deleting only the local `q -> allele selection` edge reduced the contrast to **-0.23 points** (-1.80 to +1.34). The preregistered primary difference-in-differences was therefore **+6.883 percentage points** (95% CI **+5.800 to +7.967**), resolving this single edge as a causal contributor to late functional fate in the declared closure. The associated allele-frequency, high-trait-mass and refuge contrasts also collapsed toward zero when the edge was deleted.

The resulting ecological mechanism is therefore not “alignment is good.” **q-dependent allele sorting concentrates eco-genetic compatibility in locally favourable states, while allele-linked recruitment and direct eco-genetic feedback buffer mismatch; density feedback gates the transition into system-wide loss.** Functional fate is determined by the balance of sorting and buffering through the life cycle.

This mechanism also clarifies why a marginal warning variable can respond biologically without discriminating fate. Six frozen diversity thresholds precede every observed loss in two independently seeded ensembles but also fire in every non-event, giving specificity zero and binary AUC 0.5. Detecting erosion is not equivalent to identifying whether sorting is outpacing buffering.

Published natural systems are used only to interpret these mechanisms, not to validate the finite closure. Urban *Crepis* illustrates limited buffering; Miyake-jima *Camellia–Zosterops* illustrates movement-mediated recoupling; *Conospermum* illustrates historical memory; and *Spondias* illustrates coordinated deterioration. These examples motivate prospective empirical tests of sorting, buffering and memory across fragmentation contexts.
