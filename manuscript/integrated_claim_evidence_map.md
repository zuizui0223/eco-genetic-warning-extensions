## Required wording

Use:

- “can precede,” not “always precedes”;
- “mutation or effective state-transition direction,” when discussing biological interpretation;
- “warning availability, ordering, and usable lead time,” rather than an undefined claim of performance;
- “functional-trait loss,” where the distinction from population extinction matters;
- “eco-genetic and observation closure,” when making the general conceptual claim;
- “finite Type S evidence for the declared closure,” in figure captions and limitations.

Avoid:

- describing `p_mu*` as an empirical mutation rate;
- treating the six endpoint counts as independent samples;
- combining parent and extension trajectories into a pooled statistical test;
- presenting Protocol 002 no-domain as a failed simulation or failed warning;
- presenting Protocol 003 as a retrospective modification of Protocol 002 or the parent protocol;
- claiming that connectivity, directional mutation, or genetic diversity is universally beneficial or harmful;
- presenting mutation direction as the sole novelty or sole determinant of warning reliability;
- calling a warning statistic portable without matching calibration and censoring evidence.

## Novelty test

The integrated paper is novel only if the main narrative preserves all four links:

1. ecological fragmentation and interaction state create an eco-genetic route from deterioration to functional-trait loss;
2. a diversity decline can precede trait loss under one independently calibrated closure;
3. recurrent state-transition direction reorganizes the event regimes in which warning can be evaluated;
4. independently calibrated closures differ in valid-pair availability, ordering, censoring, and lead time.

Removing link 1 reduces the work to a mutation sensitivity study. Removing link 2 removes the warning benchmark. Removing links 3–4 reduces the paper to the predecessor’s conditional result. The integrated advance is the demonstration that warning reliability emerges from the closure connecting ecological mechanism, genetic turnover, endpoint generation, calibration, and observation.
