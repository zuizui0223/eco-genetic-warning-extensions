# Cover letter draft — Ecology Letters

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Eco-genetic conditions govern when genetic early warning of functional loss can be validated,”** as a Letter in *Ecology Letters*.

Conservation monitoring increasingly seeks signals that precede loss of ecological function rather than numerical population disappearance alone. Yet a genetic statistic can only serve as an early warning when the underlying eco-genetic state generates an independently identifiable functional-loss process. We treat this as a question of **eco-genetic closure**: which future-relevant ecological and genetic state coordinates must be fixed before warning validation is well posed?

We combine a theorem-guided finite multipatch model with prospectively declared robustness, replication and natural-data tests. Fragmentation reduced interaction support, local effective size and realised high-trait mass before demographic disappearance. Warning-blind calibration recovered loss-generating states while showing that a historical allele-mixing heterogeneity signal did not freshly replicate and was not portable across more explicit movement closures. Under the declared Markov model, the complete present state is future-sufficient, whereas coarse ecological and genetic summaries can hide dynamically important cross-layer spatial alignment.

After the loss-generating state was frozen, baseline-relative genetic erosion reproducibly preceded realised functional-trait loss: all six `H_alpha/H_gamma` warning endpoints led loss in both the inherited 35/35 valid pairs and an independent fresh 33/33 ensemble. This establishes within-state reproducibility, not a universal genetic threshold. Separately calibrated states showed bounded portability rather than a single transferable warning rule. Thus, warning reliability is **not an intrinsic property of the diversity statistic**.

The natural-data extension tests the empirical counterpart of this argument. Across independent systems, upstream geographic or urban context could become predictively redundant after an informative proximal state was supplied, remain necessary when a relevant mating/connectivity coordinate was omitted, or fail to qualify as state information because the measured proxy lacked endpoint relevance. A further *Campanula* analysis showed a distinct failure mode: mechanistic efficiency information was present in the measurements but erased by feature-wise standardization. These results motivate an ordered empirical gate — **measurement adequacy -> representation/information preservation -> residual origin/history test** — before different fragmentation histories are treated as dynamically equivalent.

We believe the study fits *Ecology Letters* because its primary contribution is **ecological and conceptual rather than a model sensitivity analysis**. It provides a general framework for when early-warning claims are reproducible and transferable, linking interaction-dependent functional loss, eco-genetic state sufficiency, genetic monitoring and empirical measurement design while retaining negative and non-identifiable results as explicit boundaries rather than tuning toward a preferred warning outcome.
