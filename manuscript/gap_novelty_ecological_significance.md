# Research gap, novelty, and ecological significance

## One-sentence gap

Existing research separately addresses ecological early-warning signals, genetic monitoring, fragmentation-driven eco-evolutionary change, functional extinction, evolutionary rescue, and mutation bias, but does not test whether fragmentation-induced genetic diversity erosion warns of realised functional-trait loss, or whether the direction of recurrent variation controls the existence, reliability, censoring, and intervention time of that warning.

## One-sentence novelty

This study connects habitat geometry, interaction-dependent trait stability, finite effective population size, genetic diversity, realised functional-trait loss, and directional recurrent variation in one censoring-aware framework with warning-blind calibration and independent validation.

## One-sentence ecological importance

The results show that genetic monitoring may reveal loss of ecological function before population extinction, but actionable lead time is not a portable property of a diversity metric: it depends on the target system's spatial, interaction, and allelic or state-transition biology.

## Why the integrated paper is stronger than either component alone

The predecessor alone demonstrates a mechanism and a conditional symmetric-domain warning result, but cannot establish robustness to alternative genetic closures. The extension alone demonstrates mutation-dependent regime separation and warning degradation, but without the predecessor it risks appearing to be a parameter-sensitivity study. Together they establish a complete argument:

1. ecological fragmentation and interaction feedback create the dynamical setting in which a genetic warning can emerge;
2. a symmetric closure supplies a calibrated benchmark showing that relative diversity erosion can precede functional-trait loss;
3. directional recurrent variation changes source feasibility and partitions trait-loss regimes;
4. a common calibration domain can disappear, showing that warning-validation feasibility is itself biologically conditional;
5. after independent coordinate-specific calibration, directional transition reduces valid-pair availability, introduces lags, strengthens censoring, and shortens positive lead time.

The integration therefore changes the paper from “a warning exists in one model” or “mutation direction changes results” into “genetic warning is an emergent and non-portable property of a full eco-genetic closure.”

## Closest competing paper types

### Generic critical-slowing-down studies

These provide the conceptual warning framework but do not model genetic diversity as the warning variable or realised ecological function as the endpoint.

### Conservation-genomic monitoring studies

These establish the importance and measurability of genetic erosion, but usually do not estimate event ordering, censoring, or lead time relative to a functional-trait threshold.

### Eco-evolutionary fragmentation models

These link spatial structure, dispersal, adaptation, and persistence, but rarely produce a calibrated warning analysis and generally do not distinguish warning availability from warning ordering.

### Evolutionary-rescue models

These test whether evolution prevents extinction, whereas this study tests whether a genetic signal precedes loss of realised function and how the same processes alter warning observability.

### Mutation-bias studies

These show that the supply or accessibility of variants can affect evolutionary outcomes, but usually at the molecular substitution or adaptation level rather than at the level of ecological interaction, functional loss, and monitoring lead time.

### Functional-extinction studies

These establish that ecological roles can disappear before species extinction, but typically do not supply a finite population-genetic warning mechanism.

## The paper's strongest novelty claim

The strongest defensible claim is not that genetic diversity can decline, that fragmentation changes `N_e`, or that mutation bias matters. It is:

> The reliability of genetic warning before functional-trait loss depends on the processes generating the event regime itself. Directional recurrent variation altered not only trait persistence but also whether warning could be calibrated, whether valid warning–loss pairs existed, whether the signal led or lagged, and how much intervention time remained.

## Ecological interpretation of the main numerical contrast

The symmetric bridge produced nearly universal leading order among valid pairs and long positive lead times. This represents a regime in which erosion of diversity is both observable and temporally separated from functional loss.

The directional transition produced fewer valid pairs, non-zero ties and lags, stronger warning censoring, baseline ineligibility, and shorter positive lead times. Ecologically, this is not merely a weaker statistical signal. It means that the biological process maintaining or removing high-trait-associated states compresses the interval during which genetic monitoring can trigger action.

The rapid-loss and persistence regimes provide two additional management lessons. If loss is nearly certain and rapid, intervention may arrive too late even when a signal exists. If function is highly persistent, monitoring may show little immediate risk but warning performance is difficult to estimate because loss events are rare. The intermediate heterogeneous regime is especially challenging because pooled rates can hide non-reproducibility among historical or environmental contingencies represented by seed blocks.

## Conservation implications

### Monitor function as well as abundance

Species presence and population size do not guarantee ecological effectiveness. Monitoring programmes should identify the realised function at risk—pollination, dispersal, mutualism, defence, engineering, or another interaction-dependent contribution—and define a functional threshold explicitly.

### Interpret genetic diversity relative to mechanism

A decline in heterozygosity or allelic diversity is not inherently an early warning. Its value depends on how it relates to effective population size, trait-associated states, selection, migration, recurrent variation, and the time scale of environmental deterioration.

### Calibrate thresholds to the target system

Relative thresholds may be more transferable than absolute ones, but neither should be assumed universal. The target population's fragmentation, source state, deterioration regime, and mutation or state-transition asymmetry should inform calibration.

### Report unavailable warnings honestly

No warning, no loss, source failure, baseline ineligibility, and insufficient observation horizon are ecologically different outcomes. Censoring-aware reporting prevents apparent performance from being inflated by analysing only trajectories where both events happened.

### Preserve option value before visible collapse

Because function and genetic diversity may erode before numerical extinction, interventions that maintain connectivity, effective size, standing variation, and interaction opportunity may preserve ecological option value before conventional threat indicators become extreme.

## Limitations that sharpen rather than weaken the novelty

- `p_mu*` is an effective directional equilibrium, not a species-specific measured mutation spectrum.
- The validation contrast uses two independently calibrated domains, not all 15 coordinates.
- The results are finite Type S evidence under declared life-cycle closures.
- The six warning endpoints are correlated within trajectories.
- The study demonstrates conditional warning and conditional non-portability, not a universal law.
- Species-specific empirical validation is required before management thresholds are applied.

These limitations should be stated prominently because the scientific contribution is the discovery of conditionality and mechanism, not a claim of universality.

## Recommended title direction

**Mutation direction conditions genetic warning before functional-trait loss in fragmented populations**

Alternative with stronger mechanism emphasis:

**Eco-genetic criticality links fragmentation, directional variation, and warning before functional-trait loss**

## Recommended Introduction ending

We therefore asked whether a genetic warning that emerges from fragmentation-driven eco-genetic feedback remains reliable when recurrent transitions between high- and low-trait-associated states are directionally asymmetric. We first established the interaction and fragmentation mechanism and an independently calibrated symmetric benchmark. We then reconstructed sources across a directional mutation map, calibrated trait-loss regimes without inspecting warning outcomes, and validated relative-diversity warnings with fresh seeds in two eligible domains. This design tests not only whether genetic erosion precedes realised trait loss, but whether the biological closure determines whether warning can be observed, ordered, and acted upon.

## Recommended Discussion opening

Our results shift the interpretation of genetic early warning from a property of a diversity statistic to a property of an eco-genetic system. Fragmentation and interaction loss created conditions under which relative diversity erosion preceded realised functional-trait loss, but directional recurrent variation changed source feasibility, the frequency and reproducibility of loss events, warning censoring, lead–lag ordering, and positive lead time. Thus, the same monitoring metric can provide a long and reliable warning in one closure and a shorter, less available, occasionally lagging signal in another.
