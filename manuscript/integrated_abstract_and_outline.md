# Integrated abstract and outline

## Working title

**Mutation direction governs genetic early-warning reliability in fragmented eco-genetic systems**

## Abstract

Ecological function can be lost before population extinction, creating a need for genetic indicators that warn of realised trait loss. Yet warning performance is usually evaluated without testing whether the underlying genetic closure produces comparable loss-event regimes. We integrated a theorem-guided finite eco-genetic model of interaction feedback and fragmentation with an independently declared directional-mutation extension. In the symmetric benchmark, equal isolation reduced interaction, local effective size, and realised high-trait mass, and baseline-relative erosion of two diversity measures preceded all 35 observed trait-loss events across six preregistered endpoints. We then separated mutation-map relaxation strength from its equilibrium direction and independently reconstructed sources across 15 mutation coordinates. Of 3,375 source attempts, 2,269 were prepared and projection-supported, with coordinate-specific support ranging from 44.89% to 86.67%. Warning-blind calibration comprised 20,250 attempts: among 648 complete candidates, 322 were rapid-loss-side, 242 persistence-side, and 84 seed-heterogeneous, leaving no common eligible warning domain under the preregistered family. A separately declared protocol recovered two independently calibrated domains. The symmetric bridge yielded 323 leads, one tie, and no lags across 324 valid endpoint comparisons, whereas a directional transition yielded 184 leads, five ties, and 12 lags across 201 valid comparisons and shortened median positive lead time from 106–112 to 74–81 generations. Thus genetic diversity erosion can provide warning before functional-trait loss, but warning availability, ordering, and intervention time are conditional on mutation or effective state-transition direction. Genetic-warning thresholds should therefore be treated as properties of calibrated eco-genetic systems rather than portable statistics.

## Introduction logic

1. Functional traits and interactions may disappear before population extinction.
2. Genetic monitoring could provide advance warning, but abundance, allele presence, diversity, interaction state, and realised trait expression are distinct.
3. Existing early-warning work usually asks whether a statistic leads collapse; it less often asks whether the genetic closure creates an event regime suitable for validating that statistic.
4. The predecessor framework links fragmentation, interaction feedback, trait-state stability, local effective size, and diversity.
5. The unresolved question is whether its conditional warning result survives a change from symmetric to directional recurrent transition.
6. Hypothesis: mutation direction will partition source feasibility and trait-loss regimes and thereby alter warning availability, ordering, and lead time.

## Results logic

### Result 1 — fragmentation establishes an eco-genetic route to warning

Interaction feedback maintains a high-trait state; equal isolation lowers interaction, local effective size, and realised high-trait mass. Under one independently calibrated symmetric configuration, all observed trait-loss events were preceded by relative diversity erosion.

### Result 2 — mutation direction changes source feasibility

Across 3,375 independent attempts, source preparation and projection support varied substantially among mutation coordinates despite an unchanged ecological life cycle.

### Result 3 — mutation direction partitions trait-loss regimes

The common Protocol 002 calibration family produced rapid-loss, persistence, and seed-heterogeneous regions and no eligible warning-validation domain.

### Result 4 — directional transition weakens warning reliability

After independent domain recovery, the symmetric bridge retained near-complete leading order, whereas the directional transition showed fewer valid pairs, more censoring, non-zero lags, and shorter lead time.

## Discussion logic

### Conceptual advance

Genetic warning is not a property of a diversity statistic in isolation. It emerges from the joint ecological, demographic, genetic, and observation closure.

### Ecological importance

The findings apply most directly to interaction-dependent or functional traits whose expression may disappear before population extinction. Transition asymmetry can represent recurrent mutation, gain/loss asymmetry, epimutation, switching, or other effective state turnover.

### Monitoring implication

A transferable threshold requires evidence that the target population shares a comparable state-transition regime, deterioration timescale, baseline eligibility, and censoring structure.

### Evolutionary rescue implication

High-trait-directed transitions may support persistence but reduce the number of loss events available for validation. Low-trait-directed transitions may accelerate loss and compress the intervention window. Rescue and warning are therefore not identical objectives.

### Limits

All numerical findings are finite Type S evidence. The study does not estimate mutation rates, establish universality, or cover all directional regimes. Endpoint summaries share trajectories and must not be treated as independent replicates.
