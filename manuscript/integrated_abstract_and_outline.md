# Integrated abstract and outline

## Working title

**Eco-genetic regimes govern when genetic early warning can be validated**

## Current abstract

Ecological early-warning studies usually ask whether a signal precedes loss, but that question assumes the system generates a comparable loss regime. We used a finite eco-genetic model with warning-blind calibration, frozen endpoints and fresh validation seeds. Across a common 15-coordinate recurrent-transition grid, source support ranged from 44.89% to 86.67%, while 20,250 calibration attempts separated into rapid-loss, persistence and seed-heterogeneous regimes; strict calibration selected no common validation domain. Thus recurrent-transition direction changed whether warning could be evaluated before warning values were inspected. A separately declared recalibration recovered two non-matched eco-genetic domains. Warning availability fell from 0.540 to 0.335 and lags appeared, but direct trajectory-bootstrap differences showed endpoint-dependent absolute timing and no separated horizon-normalized timing contrast. Genetic warning therefore depends first on event-regime feasibility and only second on conditional ordering within a calibrated domain.

## Introduction logic

1. Functional traits and ecological interactions may disappear before population extinction.
2. Fragmentation can weaken interaction-dependent function and local effective size before taxonomic loss.
3. Genetic erosion could therefore provide advance warning, but warning thresholds are not guaranteed to be portable.
4. The mechanistic phase establishes the analytical interaction threshold, finite fragmentation bridge, and inherited symmetric warning benchmark; the absolute-threshold audit already shows that warning definition matters.
5. The recurrent-transition phase asks first how transition coordinates reorganise source feasibility and functional-loss regimes on a common grid.
6. Strict common-family warning-blind calibration selects no domain at any coordinate. This is the main identified extension result because it is obtained before warning outcomes are available.
7. A separately declared Protocol 003 then expands the candidate family and changes the event-risk gate before warning inspection.
8. The final two validation domains differ in transition parameters, ecological parameters, and schedules; their comparison therefore tests warning portability across calibrated eco-genetic domains rather than a single-factor direction effect.
9. Central proposition: whether warning can be evaluated is itself an eco-genetic property; conditional warning performance comes only after event-regime feasibility is established.

Present the theorem-guided interaction/fragmentation closure, the separation among potential viability, realised occupancy, local effective size, allele persistence, and diversity, the 1,055 paired H1/H3 outcomes, and the inherited symmetric warning benchmark.

Disclose Amendment 001 candidate expansion and revised pooled + four-of-five seed-block gate, the added weaker directional schedule, Amendment 002's unchanged gate plus increased replication, and the complete parameter/schedule identities of the two confirmed validation domains.

Use immutable Stage III validation artifacts only. Correct the historical Stage III upper-middle timing statistic to the conventional median, retain the 100-attempt denominator and every censoring category, resample whole trajectories for 20,000 bootstrap replicates, and plot cumulative observed warning/loss incidence. For timing contrasts, independently resample each domain and calculate the directional-minus-symmetric median difference directly for absolute, full-horizon-normalized, and hold-normalized lead time.

## Results logic

### Result 1 — fragmentation establishes the eco-genetic bridge

Across 1,055 H1-qualified paired projections, equal isolation reduced final interaction, local effective size, and realised high-trait mass in every qualified replicate; median paired reductions were 99.86%, 88.73%, and 68.87%.

### Result 2 — relative warning succeeds conditionally, not universally

In the inherited symmetric benchmark, 83/100 trajectories were available and 35 experienced functional loss; all six relative-warning endpoints led in all 35 event pairs. Fixed absolute thresholds produced lags.

### Result 3 — recurrent-transition coordinates reorganise source feasibility

Across 3,375 common-grid attempts, 2,269 completed source preparation and supported projection, with support ranging from 44.89% to 86.67%.

### Result 4 — the common deterioration family separates into event regimes

Among 648 complete Protocol 002 candidates, 322 were rapid-loss-side, 242 persistence-side, and 84 seed-heterogeneous. No candidate satisfied the strict all-seed gate; all 15 coordinates remained `no_domain_selected`. This is the core extension result: the biological closure can remove the comparable event regime required to validate a warning.

### Result 5 — warning availability and ordering differ across independently calibrated domains

Valid-pair availability among attempted endpoint opportunities was 0.540 versus 0.335. Recalibrated symmetric validation produced 323 leads, one tie and no lags; directional calibrated validation produced 184 leads, five ties and 12 lags. For directional `H_gamma` at 20%, final warning incidence was 41/81 whereas functional-trait loss incidence was 52/81.

### Result 6 — timing point estimates are schedule-dependent but uncertainty limits the contrast

Conventional median positive lead times were 106–109 versus 72.5–77.5 generations. Direct absolute directional-minus-symmetric bootstrap intervals excluded zero only for the H-alpha 5% and 10% endpoints. Full-horizon-normalized point estimates reversed to 0.442–0.454 versus 0.604–0.646, but all six normalized difference intervals included zero. Neither point-estimate ordering is therefore a general single-factor timing result.

## Discussion logic

### Event-regime feasibility comes before warning performance

The common-grid result is the strongest identified extension result. A warning cannot be tested comparably when the same deterioration family produces near-certain loss, near-complete persistence, or seed-dependent mixtures.

### Methodological discipline

Warning-blindness does not require never changing a failed design. It requires that candidate expansion and eligibility changes be declared from event-risk information before warning values are inspected, followed by independent confirmation and fresh validation.

### Why a matched schedule does not solve Stage III

The strict common-family experiment is already the matched-schedule test, and it produced no eligible validation domain at any coordinate. Coordinate-specific recalibration restored evaluability but necessarily removed single-factor identification. This trade-off is part of the biological conclusion rather than a hidden nuisance.

### Availability and censoring

A warning can lead almost always among valid pairs and still be operationally weak if valid pairs are rare. Keep source failure, baseline ineligibility, and censoring in the denominator.

### Tautology challenge

Relative warning is not proven to be a unique causal precursor. Fixed absolute thresholds, common-grid calibration failure, and nonzero Stage III lag/censoring show that ordering is not guaranteed by threshold definition alone. Future empirical tests should add negative-control variables or baseline-window perturbations.

### Biological implication

Genetic monitoring must be calibrated to the ecological function, turnover process, deterioration timescale, and observation regime that generate both warning and loss.

### Limits

All numerical findings are finite Type S evidence. Bootstrap intervals are finite-campaign uncertainty, not population-level confidence intervals. The Stage III domains are not a matched single-factor contrast and do not estimate a universal direction effect or mutation rate.
