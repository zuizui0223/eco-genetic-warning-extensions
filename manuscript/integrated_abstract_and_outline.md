# Integrated abstract and outline

## Working title

**Genetic warning emerges from eco-genetic closure in fragmented systems**

## Abstract

Ecological function can disappear before population extinction, creating a need for indicators that warn of realised functional-trait loss rather than abundance decline alone. Yet genetic-warning performance is often treated as a property of a diversity statistic, even though the ecological and genetic processes generating that statistic also determine whether comparable loss events occur. We integrated a theorem-guided finite eco-genetic model of interaction feedback and fragmentation with an independently declared directional-transition extension. Under a symmetric benchmark, equal isolation reduced interaction, local effective size, and realised high-trait mass, and baseline-relative erosion of two diversity measures preceded all 35 observed trait-loss events across six preregistered endpoints. We then separated recurrent-transition relaxation strength from equilibrium direction. Across 15 preregistered coordinates, 2,269 of 3,375 independently reconstructed sources were preparation- and projection-supported, with support ranging from 44.89% to 86.67%. Warning-blind calibration comprised 20,250 attempts: among 648 complete candidates, 322 were rapid-loss-side, 242 persistence-side, and 84 seed-heterogeneous, leaving no common eligible validation domain under the preregistered family. A separately declared protocol recovered two independently calibrated domains. The symmetric bridge yielded 323 leads, one tie, and no lags across 324 valid endpoint comparisons, whereas a directional transition yielded 184 leads, five ties, and 12 lags across 201 comparisons and shortened median positive lead time from 106–112 to 74–81 generations. Genetic warning was therefore not portable across closures: its availability, ordering, and intervention time emerged from the joint ecological, demographic, genetic, and observation system.

## Introduction logic

1. Functional traits and ecological interactions may disappear before population extinction.
2. Conservation often targets ecological function, not persistence alone.
3. Genetic monitoring could provide advance warning, but abundance, allele presence, diversity, interaction state, and realised trait expression are distinct.
4. Existing early-warning work usually asks whether a statistic leads collapse; it less often asks whether the biological closure creates an event regime suitable for validating that statistic.
5. The predecessor framework links fragmentation, interaction feedback, trait-state stability, local effective size, and diversity.
6. The unresolved question is whether its conditional warning result survives a change in recurrent state-transition direction.
7. Central proposition: genetic warning is an emergent property of eco-genetic closure rather than a portable property of a diversity statistic.

## Methods logic

### Base eco-genetic framework

Present the theorem-guided interaction and fragmentation closure, the distinction among model states, and the minimum predecessor evidence needed to establish the causal route to warning.

### Directional-transition extension

Define

\[
M(p)=\kappa_\mu p_\mu^\ast+(1-\kappa_\mu)p
\]

and explain that `kappa_mu` controls relaxation strength whereas `p_mu*` controls direction. State explicitly that the operator can represent recurrent mutation or another effective gain–loss transition and is not an empirical mutation-rate estimate.

### Evidence sequence

1. predecessor mechanism and symmetric benchmark;
2. independent source reconstruction across 15 coordinates;
3. warning-blind calibration and the preregistered no-domain rule;
4. separately declared bracket, calibration, confirmation, and fresh-seed warning validation;
5. censoring-aware endpoint analysis.

## Results logic

### Result 1 — fragmentation establishes an eco-genetic route to warning

Interaction feedback maintains a high-trait state; equal isolation lowers interaction, local effective size, and realised high-trait mass. Under one independently calibrated symmetric closure, all observed trait-loss events were preceded by relative diversity erosion.

### Result 2 — transition direction reorganizes high-trait source feasibility

Across 3,375 independent attempts, source preparation and projection support varied substantially among transition coordinates despite an unchanged ecological life cycle.

### Result 3 — eco-genetic closures separate into loss regimes

The common warning-blind calibration family produced rapid-loss, persistence, and seed-heterogeneous regions and no eligible warning-validation domain.

### Result 4 — warning reliability emerges from calibrated closure

After independent domain recovery, the symmetric bridge retained near-complete leading order, whereas the directional transition showed fewer valid pairs, more censoring, non-zero lags, and shorter lead time.

## Discussion logic

### Conceptual advance

Genetic warning is not a property of a diversity statistic in isolation. It emerges from the joint ecological, demographic, genetic, and observation closure.

### Ecological importance

The findings apply most directly to interaction-dependent or functional traits whose expression may disappear before population extinction. Transition asymmetry can represent recurrent mutation, gain–loss asymmetry, epimutation, switching, or other effective state turnover.

### Monitoring implication

A transferable threshold requires evidence that the target population shares a comparable state-transition regime, deterioration timescale, interaction feedback, baseline eligibility, and censoring structure.

### Evolutionary rescue implication

High-trait-directed transitions may support persistence but reduce the number of loss events available for validation. Low-trait-directed transitions may accelerate loss and compress the intervention window. Rescue and warning are therefore not identical objectives.

### Limits

All numerical findings are finite Type S evidence. The study does not estimate mutation rates, establish universality, or cover all directional regimes. Endpoint summaries share trajectories and must not be treated as independent replicates.
