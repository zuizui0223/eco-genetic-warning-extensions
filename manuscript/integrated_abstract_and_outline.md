# Integrated abstract and outline

## Working title

**Genetic warning emerges from eco-genetic closure in fragmented systems**

## Current abstract

Ecological function can disappear before population extinction, creating a need for genetic indicators of functional-trait loss. We used a finite eco-genetic model with warning-blind calibration, frozen endpoints and fresh validation seeds. In an inherited symmetric benchmark, relative diversity erosion preceded all 35 observed trait-loss events. Directional recurrent transitions altered source feasibility and loss regimes across a common 15-coordinate grid; strict calibration selected no common validation domain. A separately declared warning-blind protocol expanded the candidate family and used a relaxed event-risk gate before fixing two validation domains. These domains also differed in ecological parameters and deterioration schedules, so their comparison tests warning portability rather than a single transition-direction effect. Valid-pair availability was 0.540 versus 0.335; cluster-bootstrap lead fractions were 0.997 and 0.915. Absolute lead times were shorter in the directional domain, but horizon-normalized lead times were larger. Genetic warning therefore depended on calibration and the eco-genetic system.

## Introduction logic

1. Functional traits and ecological interactions may disappear before population extinction.
2. Fragmentation can weaken interaction-dependent function and local effective size before taxonomic loss.
3. Genetic erosion could therefore provide advance warning, but warning thresholds are not guaranteed to be portable.
4. The first phase establishes the analytical interaction threshold, finite fragmentation bridge, and inherited symmetric warning benchmark; the absolute-threshold audit already shows that warning definition matters.
5. The recurrent-transition phase asks first how transition coordinates reorganise source feasibility and functional-loss regimes on a common grid.
6. Strict common-family warning-blind calibration selects no domain at any coordinate.
7. A separately declared Protocol 003 then expands the candidate family and changes the event-risk gate before warning inspection.
8. The final two Stage III domains differ in transition parameters, ecological parameters, and schedules; Stage III therefore tests warning portability across calibrated eco-genetic domains rather than a single-factor direction effect.
9. Central proposition: genetic warning is an emergent property of eco-genetic closure and calibration, not a portable property of a diversity statistic.

Present the theorem-guided interaction/fragmentation closure, the separation among potential viability, realised occupancy, local effective size, allele persistence, and diversity, the 1,055 paired H1/H3 outcomes, and the inherited symmetric warning benchmark.

Disclose Amendment 001 candidate expansion and revised pooled + four-of-five seed-block gate, the added weaker directional schedule, Amendment 002's unchanged gate plus increased replication, and the complete parameter/schedule identities of the two confirmed validation domains.

Use immutable Stage III validation artifacts only. Correct the historical upper-middle timing statistic to the conventional median, normalize positive lead time by each domain's full calibrated horizon, retain the 100-attempt denominator and every censoring category, resample whole trajectories for 20,000 bootstrap replicates, and plot cumulative observed warning/loss incidence rather than classical competing risks.

## Results logic

### Result 1 — fragmentation establishes the eco-genetic bridge

Across 1,055 H1-qualified paired projections, equal isolation reduced final interaction, local effective size, and realised high-trait mass in every qualified replicate; median paired reductions were 99.86%, 88.73%, and 68.87%.

### Result 2 — relative warning succeeds conditionally, not universally

In the inherited symmetric benchmark, 83/100 trajectories were available and 35 experienced functional loss; all six relative-warning endpoints led in all 35 event pairs. Fixed absolute thresholds produced lags.

### Result 3 — recurrent-transition coordinates reorganise source feasibility

Across 3,375 common-grid attempts, 2,269 completed source preparation and supported projection, with support ranging from 44.89% to 86.67%.

### Result 4 — the common deterioration family separates into event regimes

Among 648 complete Protocol 002 candidates, 322 were rapid-loss-side, 242 persistence-side, and 84 seed-heterogeneous. No candidate satisfied the strict all-seed gate; all 15 coordinates remained `no_domain_selected`.

### Result 5 — warning availability and ordering differ across independently calibrated domains

Valid-pair availability among attempted endpoint opportunities was 0.540 versus 0.335. Recalibrated symmetric validation produced 323 leads, one tie and no lags; directional calibrated validation produced 184 leads, five ties and 12 lags. Whole-trajectory bootstrap lead fractions were 0.997 [0.990–1.000] and 0.915 [0.848–0.971].

### Result 6 — the apparent lead-time contrast is schedule-dependent

Conventional median positive lead times were 106–109 versus 72.5–77.5 generations. Because the calibrated horizons were 240 versus 120 generations, horizon-normalized medians were 0.442–0.454 versus 0.604–0.646. The absolute timing contrast therefore cannot be attributed to transition direction alone.

## Discussion logic

### Methodological advance

Warning-blindness does not require never changing a failed design. It requires that candidate expansion and eligibility changes be declared from event-risk information before warning values are inspected, followed by independent confirmation and fresh validation.

### Identification boundary

Use common-grid Stage I/II for direct recurrent-transition-coordinate claims. Treat Stage III as portability across complete calibrated eco-genetic domains.

### Availability and censoring

A warning can lead almost always among valid pairs and still be operationally weak if valid pairs are rare. Keep source failure, baseline ineligibility, and censoring in the denominator.

### Tautology challenge

Relative warning is not proven to be a unique causal precursor. Fixed absolute thresholds, common-grid calibration failure, and nonzero Stage III lag/censoring show that ordering is not guaranteed by threshold definition alone. Future empirical tests should add negative-control variables or baseline-window perturbations.

### Biological implication

Genetic monitoring must be calibrated to the ecological function, turnover process, deterioration timescale, and observation regime that generate both warning and loss.

### Limits

All numerical findings are finite Type S evidence. Bootstrap intervals are finite-campaign uncertainty, not population-level confidence intervals. Stage III is not a single-factor experiment and does not estimate a universal direction effect or mutation rate.
