# Ecological prediction requires validated state, signal and measurement

## Abstract

Ecological prediction can fail before a forecasting model is fitted because the object supplied to the forecast may itself be invalid. We combine exact results, prospectively locked finite-model experiments, a full-denominator warning audit and seven locked natural-data analyses to identify four distinct failure points. Under fragmentation, potential viability, realised occupancy, interaction support, effective size and realised trait mass did not behave as one deterioration state. States with identical common ecological and genetic marginals but opposite cross-layer alignment differed in their exact next transition by 0.2543, and a separately locked 1,500-pair experiment produced an approximately five-percentage-point functional-loss risk contrast at generations 20 and 40. Conversely, six frozen diversity thresholds preceded every observed loss in two ensembles yet also fired in every non-event, giving specificity zero and binary AUC 0.5. Natural analyses further showed missing coordinates, inadequate proxies, representation collapse and cross-study non-identifiability. These results define a predictive-validity hierarchy: ecological forecasts require explicit biological state, target-relevant representation, full-denominator signal validation and empirically adequate measurement. Success at one layer does not license the next.

Ecological forecasting is usually framed as a problem of model choice, parameter estimation and uncertainty. Yet prediction can fail before those steps begin. A structural disturbance can be summarized as though it creates one biological deterioration state, even when different biological objects respond on different schedules. A low-dimensional state vector can preserve familiar marginals while erasing spatial organisation that changes the future. A signal can consistently occur before observed events while offering no discrimination between event and non-event units. And a plausible natural proxy can be treated as a state variable before its endpoint relevance or analytical representation has been validated.

These are different errors, but they share one logical structure. A forecast requires a mapping from biological state to analytical representation, from that representation to a declared future target, and—when a warning is used—from the observed history to a predictive signal. In natural systems a further measurement map determines whether the state has actually been observed. We therefore write the forecasting problem schematically as

`biological state X_t -> representation phi(X_t) -> signal g(phi(X_0:t)) -> future target Y_(t+h)`,

with a natural measurement process supplying an estimate of the relevant components of `X_t`. None of these mappings is an identity by default.

Ecological forecasting frameworks already emphasize explicit targets, horizons and uncertainty, while multilayer ecology has shown that interactions among layers can matter beyond within-layer summaries. Early-warning theory likewise distinguishes systems in which advance signals are expected from systems in which signals may be weak, absent or stochastic. Indicator science has developed extensive principles for validation, robustness and end use. Our aim is not to replace these literatures with one universal state vector or one new warning statistic. Instead we ask a prior question: **what must be valid before ecological deterioration can be forecast?**

We answer this question using four complementary evidence layers. First, exact and finite eco-genetic results test whether one structural disturbance determines one biological state. Second, a constructive matched-summary experiment tests whether a familiar state representation preserves future-relevant information. Third, a frozen warning audit tests whether perfect temporal precedence implies prospective discrimination. Fourth, seven locked natural-data analyses test when a measured state or proxy earns the right to support residual ecological interpretation. Together these analyses establish four constructive non-implications that define a predictive-validity hierarchy.

## Results

### A common structural change did not define one biological deterioration state

We first asked whether biologically distinct quantities can be treated as interchangeable readouts of one deterioration process. In the parent eco-genetic framework, an exact positive-feedback reduction separates interaction-state geometry from a conditional high-trait viability margin and from the finite recruitment and genetic closure. The distinction is important because potential viability, realised occupancy, demographic state and genetic summaries are related by declared update rules rather than by definition.

A preregistered fixed-area fragmentation gradient provided the finite test. The same 1,037 independently prepared high-state sources were projected into one, two, three, four, six, eight, twelve or sixteen isolated equal patches. At one patch, potential high-trait viability was present in 1,037/1,037 projection-supported outcomes. At every tested subdivision from two to sixteen patches it was absent in 1,037/1,037 outcomes. Nevertheless, realised high-trait occupancy persisted at the 30-generation endpoint in approximately 99.6–100% of supported trajectories.

Other state variables followed different response shapes. At the first split into two patches, the paired median retained interaction was 0.001744 of the one-patch value, local effective size was 0.221311 and realised high-trait mass was 0.282918. Interaction and effective size then continued to decline with patch count, whereas realised high-trait mass partially recovered, reaching 0.393880 of the paired one-patch reference at sixteen patches. A common structural disturbance therefore did not generate one latent deterioration coordinate. It separated biological objects whose relevance depends on the future target being asked about.

### Matching common state summaries still produced different futures

State separation raises a second problem: even after the relevant biological objects have been named, can the chosen analytical representation erase future-relevant structure? We constructed two two-patch states that matched habitat area, census, the interaction-state multiset, allele-frequency multiset, realised high-trait-mass multiset, complete trait-bin totals, `H_alpha`, `H_gamma` and `F_ST`, while reversing the patchwise alignment between interaction support and the genetic/trait-support bundle.

The matched marginals did not imply transition equivalence. Cross-layer covariance changed from +0.025 in the aligned state to -0.025 in the anti-aligned state, and the exact generation-1 interaction transition differed patchwise by as much as **0.2543**. Thus common ecological and genetic summaries could agree while the next transition did not.

We then asked whether that hidden distinction propagated to a coarser future endpoint. A separate protocol was locked after the original state-pair result and before opening the extension outcomes. Under one fixed deterioration path, 1,500 paired aligned and anti-aligned trajectories were followed once to generation 40 and read at generations 5, 10, 20 and 40. Anti-aligned minus aligned functional-loss risk was 0.0 percentage points at generation 5 and +0.33 points at generation 10 (95% CI -0.44 to +1.11). By generation 20 it was +5.33 points (+2.04 to +8.62), and at generation 40 it was +5.20 points (+1.96 to +8.44).

The change with horizon was not inferred from a selected significance cutoff. Nested prefixes of 500, 1,000 and 1,500 pairs showed that the estimated generation-20 and generation-40 contrasts were already similar at smaller sample sizes while their intervals narrowed with replication. Under this declared deterioration path, a state distinction that was immediately visible in the transition map became detectable in cumulative functional-loss risk only at later fixed readouts. Representation adequacy was therefore target- and horizon-relative.

### Perfect temporal precedence did not imply predictive warning validity

A third failure can occur even when an apparently compelling early signal is observed. Six frozen relative-diversity thresholds—5%, 10% and 20% declines in `H_alpha` or `H_gamma` from their own baselines—had historically been evaluated by whether they preceded realised functional-trait loss among trajectories in which both warning and loss were observed.

The ordering result reproduced perfectly. In the inherited ensemble, every threshold preceded all 35 observed losses; in an independently seeded fresh ensemble, every threshold preceded all 33 observed losses. The corresponding frozen event-conditioned results were therefore 35/35 and 33/33 leads for each rule.

Restoring the full baseline-eligible denominator changed the predictive interpretation completely. The same six rules fired by the common horizon in all 48 inherited non-event trajectories and all 49 fresh non-event trajectories. Every rule in both ensembles therefore had sensitivity 1, false-positive rate 1, specificity 0 and binary-marker AUC 0.5. Positive predictive value collapsed to event prevalence.

This separation follows from an exact denominator argument. Let `Y=1` denote loss by the horizon and `M=1` denote marker firing. Perfect event-conditioned precedence forces `M=1` for every `Y=1` unit, and therefore fixes sensitivity at one, but it places no restriction on how many `Y=0` units also have `M=1`. If `f` of `n_0` non-events fire, specificity is `(n_0-f)/n_0`, and for a binary horizon marker `AUC=(1+specificity)/2`. The same perfect lead count is therefore compatible with AUC from 0.5 to 1.0. The observed ensembles occupy the sharp lower endpoint because every non-event fired.

Temporal ordering and prospective discrimination are thus different statistical objects. The result rejects these six frozen rules as predictive warnings in the tested state without implying that genetic diversity contains no predictive information generally.

### Natural measurements stopped at different validation gates

The first three analyses concern synthetic systems in which state variables are explicitly available. Natural prediction adds another failure point: the quantity supplied as a state or proxy must itself be shown to measure something relevant to the declared endpoint and must retain its intended information after preprocessing.

We applied an ordered four-gate workflow to seven locked natural-data analyses: endpoint-relevant measurement adequacy, information-preserving representation, transferable residual-context testing and cross-study identifiability. The systems were retained at their native endpoints and holdout units and were not pooled onto a common effect-size axis.

Three systems reached the residual-context gate without showing transferable gain from the added contextual variables after their locked partial states. In Honshu–Izu, held-out MSE changed from 1.08774 for the functional partial state to 1.13209 after adding mainland distance, a 4.08% worsening, with improvement at only 3/8 held-out sites. In Zurich, none of six fixed reproductive endpoints met the preregistered positive residual-context rule after function-specific pollinator interaction state was supplied. In Toronto community gardens, adding urban cover and green-space edge density worsened held-out NLL by 4932.9195, with a garden-bootstrap 95% interval of 603.9654 to 10953.6611. These are conditional residual results, not evidence that the supplied states were complete or that geography and urban context were biologically irrelevant.

Other systems failed earlier or diagnosed missing information. In *Oenothera harringtonii*, adding maternal spatial isolation after pollinator treatment reduced leave-one-maternal-plant-out MSE from 0.11619 to 0.09187, a 20.93% improvement (locked permutation `p=0.00130`), identifying a missing contemporary mating-opportunity coordinate. In *Eschscholzia californica*, an exact metadata mismatch blocked the primary seed-function mapping before model fitting and independently estimable endpoints did not provide reproducible held-out support for the preregistered pollinator proxy. In Mallorca carob, neither of two mandatory deposited pollinator-abundance representations earned endpoint-relevant adequacy for fruit production. In *Campanula americana*, independently calibrated effective-transfer predictors were constant positive rescalings of simpler visitation predictors; feature-wise standardisation made the paired coordinates identical to machine precision (maximum difference `8.88e-16`), erasing the intended efficiency weighting before prediction.

A requested cross-origin synthesis stopped at a separate identifiability gate because origin was confounded with study, taxa, protocol, state construction and response construction, and a subsequent multi-archive bridge stopped before schema mapping. The natural analyses therefore did not produce one ecological law. They showed that measurement inadequacy, missing coordinates, representation collapse and non-identifiability authorize different next inferences.

## Discussion

Across exact mathematics, prospectively locked simulations, a full-denominator warning audit and natural-data diagnostics, the same general lesson emerged at different inferential layers. A disturbance label did not identify one biological state. A set of matched marginals did not identify one future. Perfect temporal precedence did not identify predictive discrimination. And a plausible biological proxy did not automatically earn the analytical role of a validated state variable. These are four distinct failures of implication, not four demonstrations of one causal mechanism.

Their positive consequence is a hierarchy for ecological prediction. The biological object relevant to the target must first be distinguished from neighbouring states. The analytical representation must preserve distinctions that affect the declared target and horizon. A candidate warning must be evaluated on event and non-event units under a common eligibility and horizon contract. In natural data, the measurement supplied as the state must earn endpoint relevance and preserve its intended mechanistic information before residual geography, habitat, origin or history is interpreted. Success at one layer does not license the next.

This hierarchy complements quantitative ecological forecasting by locating failure modes upstream of algorithm choice. Forecast horizons already formalize how predictability changes with temporal distance; the aligned/anti-aligned experiment shows that the relevance of the **initial state representation itself** can also change with horizon. Likewise, early-warning studies often focus on whether signals appear before transitions; the denominator result shows that temporal order alone cannot determine the false-positive behaviour required for prediction. Indicator-validation frameworks emphasize relevance and robustness; the natural analyses add a fail-closed sequence in which failed measurement or representation stops stronger residual interpretation rather than being repaired after outcomes are known.

The evidence does not establish one universal eco-genetic state vector, a universal generation-20 timescale or an end-to-end natural validation of the synthetic closure. The natural systems are independent tests of empirical measurement obligations, not external replications of the aligned/anti-aligned experiment. Nor does the representation result explain why the frozen diversity thresholds failed: no warning rule was rerun with alignment information. These boundaries are essential to the synthesis because the hierarchy is one of **validation obligations**, not a claim that each observed failure caused the next.

A decisive future natural test can now be stated prospectively. Within one system, measure upstream context, local demographic/resource support, realised interaction state, partner or mating connectivity, target-relevant trait/genetic coordinates and their spatial alignment, then predict a frozen future reproductive or functional endpoint across repeated windows or an independent validation cohort. Compare context-only, coarse-marginal, joint/process-state and warning models on the same held-out denominator, and test whether origin or history adds information only after the measured state has earned endpoint relevance. Such a design would test the hierarchy end to end, regardless of whether every gate passes.

Ecological prediction therefore depends on more than choosing a good forecasting algorithm. It requires a validated chain from biological state through representation and measurement to a declared future target. Familiar summaries and early signals can be biologically meaningful while still being insufficient for the predictive claim placed on them. Making those validation layers explicit turns negative and STOP results into information about which inference is not yet licensed—and what must be measured or tested next.

## Methods

### Evidence architecture and claim ownership

The synthesis uses only claims already fixed in their owning evidence programmes. The fragmentation/state-separation results are inherited from the theorem-guided parent framework; representation and propagation results are owned by the state-validity programme; full-denominator warning validity is owned by the frozen warning audit; natural measurement and identifiability results are owned by the independent four-gate natural-data programme. No result is reclassified as evidence for a causal link that its owning protocol did not test.

### Fragmentation-gradient experiment

The finite fragmentation gradient used 1,200 attempted fresh source replicates, of which 1,037 completed the required high-state source preparation. Each supported source was projected, under the same fixed total area and source-state preservation contract, into 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 16 isolated equal patches. The analysis retained all projection-supported outcomes and reports paired state ratios relative to the one-patch representation. Potential viability and realised occupancy were tracked separately.

### Constructive representation and propagation experiment

The constructive state pair matched the declared coarse ecological and genetic marginals while reversing cross-layer alignment. The primary certificate was the exact next interaction transition. A later, independently prospectively locked experiment followed 1,500 paired trajectories under one common deterioration path to generation 40 and read cumulative functional loss at generations 5, 10, 20 and 40. The primary estimand at each horizon was anti-aligned minus aligned paired risk difference with a paired 95% interval. Nested 500- and 1,000-pair prefixes were precision diagnostics.

### Full-denominator warning audit

The six frozen markers were first post-baseline generations at which `H_alpha` or `H_gamma` declined 5%, 10% or 20% from its own baseline. The inherited ensemble retained 83 baseline-eligible trajectories (35 losses, 48 horizon non-events); the independently seeded fresh ensemble retained 82 (33 losses, 49 non-events). Warning thresholds, eligibility, horizon and event definition were not recalibrated. The audit restored all baseline-eligible trajectories and calculated horizon sensitivity, specificity, false-positive rate, predictive values and binary-marker AUC.

### Natural-data gate analyses

The natural-data programme fixed each source study, endpoint, candidate state/proxy and ecological holdout unit before the corresponding locked analysis. Holdouts remained whole sites, gardens, maternal plants, arrays, orchards or populations as defined in the original protocols. The synthesis is logical rather than meta-analytic: endpoints and loss functions remain on their native scales. Gate failures, metadata STOPs, failed adequacy, representation collapse and cross-study non-identifiability were retained rather than repaired through endpoint switching, proxy substitution or post-result rescaling.

### Reproducibility

All four evidence programmes retain machine-readable registries, preregistrations or protocols, locked result summaries and reproducible submission artifacts in their authoritative repositories. The flagship does not alter those source-of-truth files. A dedicated flagship bundle will pin exact repository commits and regenerate the five main displays from those locked evidence objects.
