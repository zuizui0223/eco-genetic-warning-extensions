# Main figure captions

## Figure 1. Eco-genetic causal architecture from function to warning

Conceptual synthesis separating functional-state feasibility, structural and interaction-mediated fragmentation, network/interaction state, realised ecological function, the distribution of functional-loss events, a finite validation certificate and genetic-warning performance. The study asks first whether function can exist and be lost, then how recurrent turnover, connectivity and interaction processes shape central loss incidence and among-block heterogeneity, and only then whether a specified finite panel is suitable for genetic-warning validation. The historical R1/R2/R3/R4 labels are sampling-design classifications, not warning outcomes or universal biological states.

## Figure 2. Fragmentation creates vulnerability and genetic warning is conditionally possible

**A**, paired effect of equal isolation versus the matched one-large projection across 1,055 H1-qualified full-state sources, summarized for final interaction, local effective size and realised high-trait mass. **B**, inherited symmetric warning benchmark: baseline-relative `H_alpha` and `H_gamma` erosion at 5%, 10% and 20% preceded all 35 observed realised functional-loss pairs, whereas the predeclared absolute thresholds produced both leads and lags. These are finite results under the declared parent closure; they do not establish a universal genetic-warning rule.

## Figure 3. Recurrent state turnover reorganises source feasibility and functional-loss outcomes

**A**, projection-supported source fractions across the common 15-coordinate recurrent-transition grid; support ranged from 44.89% to 86.67%. **B**, warning-blind loss outcomes from 648 complete five-seed candidates: 322 rapid-loss, 84 seed-heterogeneous and 242 persistence candidates. The original coarse grid contained no strict finite R4 certificate and all 15 coordinates remain historically `no_domain_selected`. This is a result for that candidate family, not warning failure or structural impossibility.

## Figure 4. Finite calibration certificates and their sampling boundary

**A**, historical warning-blind frontier recovery. `p_star=.35` independently satisfied the finite R4 all-block rule twice, whereas immediate tested neighbours were R3 in those high-rep panels. The shaded `[.30,.70]` interval is the predeclared operational incidence band and not a universal ecological threshold. **B**, Phase-J stability audit at one fixed `m=.10` biological condition. Four prospectively fixed five-seed panels classified R4/R3/R4/R4; 19/20 fresh block rates were inside the operational band and one was `.75`. Across all `C(20,5)=15,504` five-block combinations, 11,628 (75%) are R4 and 3,876 (25%) R3. Thus R4 is a finite-panel calibration certificate whose pass probability depends on sampling design, not a sample-size-invariant biological regime.

## Figure 5. Connectivity and interaction processes reshape different layers of functional loss

**A**, Phase-E allele-frequency connectivity. Paired functional-loss status switched in both directions as `m` increased; the original five-seed campaign labelled `m=.10/.20` R3, but independent Phase-I/J ensembles show that the exact categorical `m=.10` boundary is not portable. **B**, Phase-F aggregate interaction support: source/baseline eligibility differed across `kappa=3,4.5,6` while all 15 tested seed-block rates lay in the operational intermediate-risk band. **C**, Phase-G/H interaction structure. One-partner loss broadened among-block outcomes without a detectable mean-risk shift; constrained rewiring increased active edges from 3 to 5, realised connectance from .500 to .833 and final support multiplier from .750 to .844 while pooled loss changed only .430→.419. **D**, Phase-I process-resolved pollen movement. Regional pollen `g=.20` was snapshot-exact with legacy global mixing `m=.10` in 90/90 pairs, whereas regional versus ring pollen reshuffled loss status bidirectionally but shared the same finite certificate. Network state, process-resolved connectivity, mean loss incidence and among-block heterogeneity are therefore not interchangeable.

## Figure 6. Portability after loss-process characterisation

**A**, full attempted-denominator warning availability, censoring and lead/tie/lag composition for the independently recalibrated symmetric and directional calibrated domains. **B**, cumulative observed warning and realised functional-loss incidence over each domain-specific administrative horizon for the compact endpoint set retained in the main figure. Warning and loss can both occur in one trajectory, so incidence curves are paired event summaries rather than competing-risk estimates. **Endpoint rows share trajectories** and are not independent biological replicates. The two domains differ in recurrent-transition, ecological and deterioration parameters; Stage III therefore tests bounded portability across calibrated eco-genetic domains and does not identify an isolated causal effect of recurrent-transition direction.

## Supplementary Figure S3. Conditional positive lead-time diagnostic

Positive warning lead time is summarized only among trajectories in which both the warning endpoint and realised functional loss were observed and the warning preceded loss. This conditional diagnostic is secondary to full-denominator warning availability and event incidence. Absolute-generation lead times depend on each domain's calibrated horizon; horizon-normalized direct between-domain bootstrap intervals include zero for all six endpoints.
