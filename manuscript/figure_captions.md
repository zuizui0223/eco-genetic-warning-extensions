# Main figure captions

## Figure 1. Genetic warning emerges from eco-genetic closure

Fragmentation alters interaction state, realised functional-trait occupancy, local demography, and genetic state; relative diversity erosion is observed within this coupled system rather than generated independently of it. Mutation or effective state-transition direction, deterioration, migration, baseline eligibility, calibration, and censoring modify both the warning signal and the functional-trait-loss endpoint. Arrows show the tested causal organization, not a universal theorem. This panel is a conceptual synthesis; numerical results in subsequent figures are finite Type S evidence for the declared closures.

## Figure 2. High-trait source feasibility depends on transition closure

Fraction of independently reconstructed source attempts that completed source preparation and passed all projection invariants across the 15 combinations of transition-map relaxation strength (`kappa_mu`) and transition-only equilibrium (`p_star`). Support generally increased towards high-trait-directed transition equilibria, showing that transition direction changes whether the ecological starting state can be established before deterioration is imposed. Each coordinate contains 225 attempts. The display summarizes the independently declared source-reconstruction campaign and is finite Type S evidence for the tested closure.

## Figure 3. Transition direction separates rapid-loss, persistence, and seed-heterogeneous regimes

Warning-blind trait-loss outcomes across the full transition-coordinate map. Candidate domains were classified from seed-block trait-loss frequencies without access to diversity trajectories, warning times, lead–lag ordering, or usable lead time. Low transition equilibria were dominated by rapid functional-trait loss, high equilibria at stronger relaxation by persistence, and intermediate coordinates often by seed heterogeneity. No coordinate contained a candidate satisfying the declared all-seed eligibility rule. The no-domain outcome is evidence about event-regime and validation feasibility, not evidence that warning failed.

## Figure 4. Pooled event frequency can conceal opposing seed-level regimes

Composition of complete calibration candidates within each transition coordinate, partitioned into rapid-loss, persistence, and seed-heterogeneous classes. Seed-heterogeneous candidates cross the declared eligibility band among seed blocks even when their pooled frequency appears suitable. The figure therefore tests whether a warning-validation domain is reproducible across stochastic backgrounds rather than merely acceptable after pooling. Counts derive from 648 complete candidates in the warning-blind calibration campaign.

## Figure 5. Warning ordering is closure-dependent after independent calibration

Lead, tie, lag, warning-censored, trait-loss-censored, and baseline-ineligible outcomes for six preregistered relative-diversity endpoints in two independently calibrated demonstration domains. The symmetric bridge produced near-uniform leading order, whereas the directional transition produced fewer valid pairs, non-zero ties and lags, and greater censoring. Endpoint records share trajectories and are correlated; counts are descriptive outcomes, not independent biological replicates. The two domains demonstrate closure dependence but do not represent the entire 15-coordinate map.

## Figure 6. Directional transition shortens usable intervention time

Median positive time between the first baseline-relative diversity warning and realised functional-trait loss for each preregistered endpoint. Positive lead times were longer in the symmetric bridge than in the directional transition, which also showed lower valid-pair availability and non-zero lag. Medians are conditional on an observed leading warning–loss pair and must be interpreted together with Figure 5 censoring and eligibility outcomes. Results are finite Type S evidence for the two independently calibrated domains.
