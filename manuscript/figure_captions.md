# Main figure captions

## Figure 1. Genetic warning emerges from eco-genetic closure

Fragmentation alters interaction state, high-trait state, local effective size, and genetic diversity; relative diversity erosion is observed within this coupled system rather than generated independently of it. Recurrent-transition direction, deterioration, migration, baseline eligibility, calibration, and censoring modify the closure. Arrows show the tested causal organization, not a universal theorem. Numerical results in subsequent figures are finite Type S evidence for declared model closures.

## Figure 2. High-trait source feasibility depends on recurrent-transition coordinates

Fraction of independently reconstructed source attempts that completed source preparation and passed all projection invariants across the 15 combinations of transition-map relaxation strength (`kappa_mu`) and transition-only equilibrium (`p_star`). Each coordinate contains 225 attempts. Within fixed-`kappa_mu` rows, support generally increased towards high-trait-directed equilibria. This common-grid comparison precedes deterioration calibration and is the direct evidence that recurrent-transition direction reorganises source feasibility.

## Figure 3. The common deterioration family separates rapid-loss, heterogeneous, and persistence regimes

**A**, Warning-blind trait-loss outcomes across the full 15-coordinate transition map. Each cell shows the dominant seed-block regime, the closest pooled trait-loss frequency, and the number of complete candidates. **B**, Composition of all 648 complete candidates: 322 rapid-loss-side, 84 seed-heterogeneous, and 242 persistence-side. Calibration used realised trait loss only; diversity trajectories, warning times, ordering, and lead time were unavailable. No coordinate contained a candidate satisfying the strict Protocol 002 all-seed eligibility rule, so all 15 were recorded as `no_domain_selected`. This is evidence about event-regime and validation feasibility, not warning failure.

## Figure 4. Cumulative warning and functional-loss incidence across calibrated horizons

Cumulative observed incidence among baseline-eligible completed Stage III trajectories. Rows show `H_alpha` and `H_gamma`; columns show the **recalibrated symmetric domain** and **directional calibrated domain**. Within each panel, the three relative-warning curves correspond to 5%, 10%, and 20% decline from the trajectory's own baseline; the black curve is realised functional-trait loss. Non-events remain in the denominator through administrative censoring at the domain-specific horizon. The recalibrated symmetric domain has a 240-generation horizon (30-generation ramp + 210-generation hold), whereas the directional calibrated domain has a 120-generation horizon (30-generation ramp + 90-generation hold). Because warning and trait loss can both occur in one trajectory, these are paired cumulative event-incidence curves rather than classical competing-risk estimates.

## Figure 5. Warning availability, censoring, and ordering across the full attempted denominator

Each horizontal bar represents 100 attempted trajectories for one preregistered warning endpoint. Segments retain source-preparation failure (SF), baseline ineligibility (BI), both events censored (BC), warning censoring (WC), trait-loss censoring (TC), lead, tie, and lag. Unlike a valid-pairs-only display, every bar keeps the attempted denominator fixed, so differences in warning availability remain visible. The six endpoint rows within a trajectory are correlated; ordering fractions and uncertainty reported in the text use trajectory-cluster resampling rather than treating the endpoint rows as independent observations.

## Figure 6. Absolute and horizon-normalized positive warning lead time

Conventional median positive time from first baseline-relative warning to realised functional-trait loss for each endpoint, with percentile 95% intervals from 20,000 bootstrap resamples of whole trajectories. Point labels give the number of leading trajectories contributing to each median. **A**, Absolute lead time in generations. **B**, The same lead times divided by the full calibrated deterioration horizon (ramp + hold). The recalibrated symmetric domain uses `A_ref=0.8`, interaction-feedback `kappa=6.0`, `kappa_mu=0.20`, `p_star=0.50`, horizon 240, and normalized barrier increase 0.20. The directional calibrated domain uses `A_ref=1.0`, `kappa=4.5`, `kappa_mu=0.05`, `p_star=0.90`, horizon 120, and normalized barrier increase 0.10. Absolute medians are shorter in the directional calibrated domain, whereas horizon-normalized medians are larger. Stage III therefore does not identify a single-factor effect of recurrent-transition direction on intervention time.
