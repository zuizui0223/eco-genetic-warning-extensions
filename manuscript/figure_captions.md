# Main figure captions

## Figure 1. Eco-genetic causal architecture and four-question hierarchy

Conceptual synthesis separating interaction-supported ecological function, population persistence, genetic state, functional loss, event-regime estimability and genetic-warning performance. The study asks first whether function can exist and be lost, then whether loss is reproducible and nondegenerate enough for warning to be estimated, and only then whether genetic change precedes loss and is portable. Arrows summarize the tested organization; they do not assert a universal theorem.

## Figure 2. Fragmentation creates vulnerability and genetic warning is conditionally possible

**A**, paired first-phase effect of equal isolation versus the matched one-large projection across 1,055 H1-qualified full-state sources, summarized for final interaction, local effective size and realised high-trait mass. **B**, inherited symmetric warning benchmark: baseline-relative `H_alpha` and `H_gamma` erosion at 5%, 10% and 20% preceded all 35 observed realised functional-loss pairs, whereas the predeclared absolute thresholds produced both leads and lags. These are finite results under the declared parent closure; they do not establish a universal genetic-warning rule.

## Figure 3. Recurrent state turnover reorganises source feasibility and functional-loss regime

**A**, projection-supported source fractions across the common 15-coordinate recurrent-transition grid. Source support ranged from 44.89% to 86.67%. **B**, warning-blind functional-loss regime structure from 648 complete five-seed candidates generated by the common deterioration family: 322 rapid-loss, 84 seed-heterogeneous and 242 persistence candidates. The original coarse grid contained no strict R4 candidate and retained 15/15 `no_domain_selected`; this is a historical finite result for that candidate family, not warning failure or structural impossibility.

## Figure 4. Warning-blind recovery of a narrow reproducible event regime

**A**, fixed B1 recurrent-transition frontier: historical rapid loss at `p_star=0.25`, persistence at `0.50`, and the Phase-B pooled-loss gradient across `0.30, 0.35, 0.40, 0.45`. **B**, high-rep seed-block functional-loss rates from Phases C and D. `p_star=0.35` independently satisfied the R4 criterion twice, whereas `0.325`, `0.375` and the Phase-C `0.40` cell were R3. The shaded `[0.30,0.70]` interval is the predeclared operational event-risk band used to define R4; it is not a universal ecological threshold. Warning and diversity outcomes were unavailable during condition recovery.

## Figure 5. Effective genetic connectivity changes event-regime estimability

At the independently reproduced R4 anchor, the same 100 prepared sources were paired across allele-frequency migration rates. **A**, pooled and independent seed-block functional-loss rates at `m=0, 0.025, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20`; the first three conditions were R4-highrep and the latter two R3-highrep. **B**, paired loss-status switches relative to isolation among the 91 sources comparable across all levels, separated into `loss→no loss` and `no loss→loss`. Both directions occurred at every nonzero rate. `migration_rate` is allele-frequency mixing toward the population-weighted selected mean; it is not demographic migration, pollinator movement, seed dispersal or recolonisation. No tested value is interpreted as a universal connectivity threshold.

## Figure 6. Portability after evaluability is separately recovered

**A**, full attempted-denominator warning availability, censoring and lead/tie/lag composition for the independently recalibrated symmetric and directional calibrated domains. **B**, cumulative observed warning and realised functional-loss incidence over each domain-specific administrative horizon for the compact endpoint set retained in the main figure. Warning and loss can both occur in one trajectory, so incidence curves are paired event summaries rather than competing-risk estimates. The two domains differ in recurrent-transition, ecological and deterioration parameters; Stage III therefore tests bounded portability across calibrated eco-genetic domains and does not identify the isolated causal effect of recurrent-transition direction.
