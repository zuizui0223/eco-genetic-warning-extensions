# Prior-art boundary for warning evaluability

## Purpose

Prevent the condition-map storyline from claiming novelty for ideas that already exist in early-warning and critical-transition ecology.

## Already established or closely adjacent

### Different transition mechanisms can produce different early-warning behavior

This is well established by the classical and post-classical EWS literature. Some stochastic/regime transitions need not show classical critical-slowing-down warnings, and detectability depends on mechanism, noise, sampling and the state variable observed.

### Ecological parameter space can contain different routes to extinction

Spatial population models have already mapped qualitatively different extinction routes across parameter space. For example, Storch & Day (2022) found two dominant paths to extinction whose occurrence depended on dispersal and the rate of parameter drift, and used route-specific topology to construct alternative early-warning indicators.

Therefore the present study must not claim novelty merely for showing "different regimes in parameter space" or "different routes to loss."

### Stochastic systems can have uncertain tipping regions rather than one deterministic threshold

Recent work has explicitly generalized deterministic tipping points to stochastic **tipping sets**. In uncertain systems, regime membership can be defined through the probability/propensity of remaining in or leaving a satisfaction set, and randomness can broaden the transition region. Thus the presence of a seed-heterogeneous or probabilistic frontier is not, by itself, a novel conceptual contribution.

This is especially relevant to Phase A/B: an interior frontier occupied by heterogeneous event rates should not be advertised simply as discovery of a stochastic tipping region.

### The domain of mechanistic models underlying EWS is an explicit research problem

Recent EWS work discusses the need to characterize which mechanistic model classes produce catastrophic transitions and whether warnings remain valid under different environmental rates/mechanisms. Arumugam et al. (2024), for example, explicitly frame extension of the mechanistic model domain as part of EWS applicability.

Therefore the present study must not claim to invent mechanism-aware EWS evaluation.

## Narrower contribution supported by the current study

The candidate contribution is the **ordered identification layer** created by the finite eco-genetic design:

```text
source feasibility
      ↓
functional-loss event regime
      ↓
matched event-regime evaluability
      ↓
warning availability / ordering / timing
```

The key operational distinction is that the third layer is determined **before the proposed genetic warning is available to calibration**. A candidate system is not entered into a warning-performance comparison unless the functional-loss process itself is reproducible and nondegenerate under the predeclared gate.

Protocol 002 and the later warning-blind frontier refinements therefore do more than demonstrate heterogeneous EWS performance: they prevent warning performance from being promoted to the primary comparison when the event-generating prerequisite is absent.

Phase B sharpens this point. Along a matched `p_star` bracket, pooled functional-loss rate moves smoothly from 1 toward 0, but every interior cell is seed-heterogeneous under the predeclared all-seed rule. Thus **intermediate pooled event risk and reproducible warning-evaluable event regime are different estimands**.

That distinction, rather than stochasticity itself, is the load-bearing conceptual result to test further.

## Novelty wording to prefer

Avoid:

- "We introduce ecological phase maps for early warning."
- "We show for the first time that EWS depend on mechanism."
- "We discover that there are different routes to collapse."
- "We discover a stochastic tipping region."
- "We discover that diversity and function are context dependent."

Prefer:

> **We treat the existence of a reproducible event regime as a pre-warning estimability condition and map that condition independently of the proposed genetic warning.**

Or, more specifically after Phase B:

> **We distinguish an intermediate pooled probability of functional loss from a reproducible event regime in which warning performance is actually estimable across independent stochastic realizations.**

Or, biologically:

> **We separate the eco-genetic conditions that generate a functional state and a reproducible loss process from the downstream question of whether genetic change precedes that loss.**

The contribution remains finite/model-specific until replicated in broader models or empirical systems.

## Why the urban/island applications matter for this novelty test

Urban and island systems can test whether the evaluability layer adds information beyond generic fragmentation/transition classifications.

- In cities, similar spatial fragmentation can yield high pollen connectivity or effective functional isolation depending on pollinator movement and species specialization.
- On islands, strong geographic isolation can favour reproductive assurance but can also retain obligate outcrossing when an effective pollination niche exists.

These systems therefore create repeated cases where a coarse label (urban, isolated island, fragmented) does not uniquely determine the event-generating regime. The proposed empirical test is to classify source/function-loss regimes and their reproducibility first, then test genetic warning only inside replicated nondegenerate regimes.
