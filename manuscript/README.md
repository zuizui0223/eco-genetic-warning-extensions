# Manuscript workspace

**Working title:** *Directional recurrent mutation partitions trait-loss regimes and genetic-warning reliability in finite eco-genetic systems*

## Paper identity

This is a companion paper to `zuizui0223/eco-genetic-criticality`, not a replacement or silent revision of its closed evidence ledger. The predecessor establishes a theorem-guided finite eco-genetic framework and a conditional relative-diversity warning result under a symmetric-mutation closure. This extension asks how mutation direction changes source feasibility, realised trait-loss regimes, warning-domain availability, and relative-warning performance.

The repositories remain separate. The predecessor is cited as prior framework and bridge control; its trajectories and selected domain are not evidence for this paper.

## Primary claim

At fixed mutation-map relaxation strength, changing the mutation-only equilibrium partitions the declared finite eco-genetic system into rapid-loss, persistent, and seed-heterogeneous regimes. Under the preregistered Protocol 002 family, this partition eliminated every warning-validation domain. A separately declared Protocol 003 recovered two domains with independent calibration and showed that mutation direction changed warning availability and ordering: the symmetric bridge produced 323 leads, one tie, and zero lags across six endpoints, whereas the directional transition produced 184 leads, five ties, and 12 lags with stronger censoring.

## Claims not made

- This paper does not establish a universal genetic-warning theorem.
- It does not estimate biological mutation rates.
- `no_domain_selected` is not interpreted as absence of ecological change.
- Protocol 003 results are not retroactively attributed to Protocol 002.
- Endpoint-level counts are correlated within trajectories and are not treated as independent replicates.
- Finite-model results remain Type S evidence for the declared closure.

## Evidence completed

- Stage 0: algebraic and implementation certificate for 15 mutation coordinates.
- Stage I: 3,375 independent source-reconstruction/projection attempts.
- Stage II: 20,250 trait-loss-only calibration attempts in 810 candidate batches.
- Protocol 002 selection: 648 complete five-seed candidates, zero eligible candidates, and 15/15 coordinates recorded as `no_domain_selected`.
- Diagnostic audit: 322 complete candidates above the eligibility band, 242 below it, and 84 mixed across it.
- Protocol 003 bracket, independent calibration, and confirmation calibration with disjoint seed families.
- Stage III: 200 fresh-seed validation trajectories in two confirmation-eligible domains and six preregistered warning endpoints.

## Manuscript structure

1. Introduction — mutation direction as an ecological boundary condition, not a generic sensitivity parameter.
2. Model — separation of relaxation strength and mutation-only equilibrium.
3. Protocol — independent source reconstruction, warning-blind calibration, censoring, and protocol amendments.
4. Results I — source feasibility and projection support across the mutation-coordinate map.
5. Results II — trait-loss regime separation and Protocol 002 no-domain outcome.
6. Results III — Protocol 003 recovery of two domains and mutation-dependent warning availability, lead/tie/lag ordering, and lead time.
7. Discussion — implications for evolutionary rescue, functional-trait loss, genetic monitoring, and limits of universal warning rules.
