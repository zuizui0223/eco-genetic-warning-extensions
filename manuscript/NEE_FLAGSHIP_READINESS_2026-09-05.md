## Reader-facing identity

**Title:** *Ecological prediction requires validated state, signal and measurement*

**Central question:**

> What must be valid before ecological deterioration can be forecast?

**Positive answer:**

> Ecological predictability depends on a validated chain from biological state, through target-relevant representation and full-denominator signal validation, to empirically adequate measurement. Success at one layer does not license the next.

The Article is not organized by repository or by historical campaign. It is organized by four constructive non-implications:

1. common disturbance != one biological state;
2. matching marginals != matching futures;
3. perfect precedence != predictive discrimination;
4. plausible proxy != validated empirical state.

## Nearest-neighbour novelty boundary

The closest recent Nature Ecology & Evolution neighbours sharpen rather than erase the manuscript's niche:

- Song & Levine (2025) test the validity of ecological **model equations** against empirical time-series covariance relationships.
- Ovaskainen et al. (2026) demonstrate scalable real-time biodiversity forecasting and independent predictive validation.
- The present flagship asks a logically prior question: **whether the biological state, analytical representation, warning signal and empirical measurement supplied to a forecast are themselves valid for the declared target and horizon.**

The manuscript does not claim priority over the separate literatures on state definition, forecast horizon, warning false positives or indicator validation. Its novelty is the constructive testing and integration of four non-implications into one predictive-validity hierarchy.

## Principal scientific ceiling

The largest remaining limitation is explicit and singular: **there is no one natural system that prospectively traverses all four validation layers end to end.**

The seven natural analyses test empirical measurement obligations independently; they do not externally validate the finite simulator. The prospective Cirsium bridge protocol defines the strongest future ceiling-raising experiment, but it is not retrospectively used as current evidence and is not automatically required before the first NEE shot.
