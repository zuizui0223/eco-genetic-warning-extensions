# Introduction

Ecological deterioration can eliminate an interaction-dependent trait before the population carrying it goes extinct. This distinction matters because conservation often seeks to maintain pollination, dispersal, defence, mutualism, or other ecological processes rather than population persistence alone (Soulé et al. 2005; McConkey & Drake 2006; Valiente-Banuet et al. 2015). Abundance, allele presence, genetic diversity, interaction state, and realised trait occupancy are therefore distinct monitoring targets.

Early-warning research asks whether a statistic changes before collapse (Scheffer et al. 2009; Drake & Griffen 2010), whereas genetic monitoring asks whether changes in diversity or allele frequency reveal deterioration before demographic or functional loss (Schwartz et al. 2007; Stange et al. 2021). Both can overlook a more fundamental dependency: warning depends on mechanism, observation, event definition, and the existence of comparable loss events and need not be universal across systems (Hastings & Wysham 2010; Boettiger & Hastings 2012, 2013).

Habitat fragmentation provides a direct route by which these states can become uncoupled. Spatial structure jointly alters demography, interactions, gene flow, drift, and adaptation (Legrand et al. 2017; Govaert et al. 2019). In the first phase of this study, a theorem-guided interaction model was linked to a finite fragmentation experiment in which the same prepared high-trait state was projected into one-large and equal-isolated landscapes. Under one independently calibrated symmetric benchmark, baseline-relative diversity erosion preceded every observed functional-trait loss, whereas fixed absolute diversity thresholds produced both leads and lags. The first phase therefore established that genetic warning can exist but is conditional on the warning definition and declared life-cycle closure.

The recurrent-transition phase asks whether that conditional warning remains portable when the processes supplying and removing high-trait-associated states are changed. Recurrent transition direction can shape evolutionary outcomes through its interaction with selection, demography, and the supply of alternatives (Stoltzfus & McCandlish 2017; Storz et al. 2019). We first varied transition coordinates across a common 15-coordinate grid while retaining the same source-reconstruction and deterioration families. This directly tests how transition direction reorganises high-trait source feasibility and realised functional-loss regimes.

Strict warning-blind calibration over that common grid selected no validation domain. Rather than expanding Protocol 002 after seeing warning results, we closed it with 15/15 `no_domain_selected` and opened a separately declared Protocol 003. That protocol expanded the candidate schedules and used a different event-risk gate based only on trait-loss and baseline-eligibility information before any warning endpoint was calculated. Independent confirmation and fresh validation seeds were then used after the two domains were frozen.

The resulting Stage III domains are not a matched single-factor experiment. The recalibrated symmetric and directional calibrated domains differ in `A_ref`, interaction-feedback `kappa`, `kappa_mu`, `p_star`, normalized barrier increase, and calibrated horizon. Their comparison therefore asks whether the same relative-diversity warning remains operational across two independently calibrated eco-genetic domains. It does not identify the isolated causal contribution of transition direction to warning ordering or intervention time.

This study thus asks a broader question than whether genetic diversity can decline before functional loss. We ask whether the biological and observational system creates a usable warning regime, how recurrent-transition coordinates reorganise that regime on a common grid, and whether a warning definition remains portable after independent event-risk calibration. Our central proposition is that genetic-warning reliability is an emergent property of the complete eco-genetic closure and that useful warning thresholds are not portable statistics independent of the systems that generate them.
