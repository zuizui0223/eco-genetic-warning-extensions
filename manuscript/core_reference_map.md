## Citation and novelty boundaries

- Cite the predecessor repository or its eventual manuscript for inherited H1, H3 and symmetric H2-R numerical results.
- Cite the extension repository / integrated data package for C2–C4 numerical results.
- Do not use external literature to imply that the exact Phase-E, Phase-F or Phase-G result has already been demonstrated empirically.
- Do not claim that genetic early warning under fragmentation is unprecedented; Peled et al. (2026) is a close predecessor whose endpoint is a rapid **genetic** transition. The present distinction is the **interaction-dependent functional endpoint plus warning-blind recovery of event estimability**.
- Do not equate structural fragmentation with network simplification. Gama et al. (2025) directly shows a case where habitat loss, rather than fragmentation per se, drives network restructuring.
- Do not use `functional fragmentation` as an unqualified novelty label. Existing functional fragmentation/connectivity language is established; use `interaction-mediated functional fragmentation` when naming the present process.
- Do not infer urban genetic isolation from urban patchiness. Hardion et al. (2026) specifically challenges that assumption.
- Do not infer one island mutualism rule from Delavaux et al. (2024); retain the critique/response caveat.
- Do not describe `p_mu*` as an observed mutation spectrum or allele-frequency `migration_rate` as demographic, pollen, seed or pollinator movement.
- Use reviews to orient broad fields, but retain original manipulative/empirical studies for central claims about species loss, rewiring and realised function.
