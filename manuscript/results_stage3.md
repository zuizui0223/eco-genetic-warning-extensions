# Results III — mutation direction changes warning availability and ordering

Fresh-seed validation was run in the two Protocol 003 domains that passed confirmation calibration. Each domain contained five new master seeds and 20 replicates per seed, for 100 attempted trajectories per domain. All six preregistered relative-warning endpoints were retained: 5%, 10%, and 20% post-baseline declines in H-alpha and H-gamma.

## Symmetric bridge

The symmetric bridge domain completed 82 of 100 attempted trajectories. Every completed trajectory had an eligible diversity baseline. Fifty-four trajectories contained a trait-loss event and produced a valid warning/loss pair for every endpoint. Relative diversity decline preceded trait loss in 54 of 54 valid pairs for five endpoints. For the 20% H-gamma endpoint, 53 pairs led and one tied; no endpoint produced a lag. Median positive lead time ranged from 106 to 112 generations. Across the six endpoints, the finite validation produced 323 leads, one tie, and zero lags among 324 valid endpoint-level comparisons.

## Directional transition

The directional transition domain completed 91 of 100 attempted trajectories, but warning availability was lower and more endpoint-dependent. Ten completed trajectories had ineligible diversity baselines. Valid-pair counts declined from 38 for a 5% H-alpha decline to 28 for a 20% H-gamma decline. Lead fractions among valid pairs ranged from 0.857 to 0.971. Across all endpoints, there were 184 leads, five ties, and 12 lags among 201 valid endpoint-level comparisons. Median positive lead time ranged from 74 to 81 generations. Warning censoring increased with threshold severity, particularly for H-gamma, reaching 24 trajectories at the 20% endpoint.

## Between-domain interpretation

The result is not a universal warning theorem. It is finite Type S evidence under two independently calibrated domains. Within that declared closure, the symmetric bridge produced nearly deterministic lead ordering once a valid event pair existed, whereas the directional transition reduced baseline eligibility and valid-pair availability and admitted nonzero lag probability. Mutation direction therefore affected not only the trait-loss regime used for calibration, but also whether relative-diversity warning was observable and how reliably it preceded realised trait loss.
