# Eco-genetic warning extensions

This repository tests **when an eco-genetic warning question is biologically well posed**. It is the condition-recovery extension of [`eco-genetic-criticality`](https://github.com/zuizui0223/eco-genetic-criticality), pinned for inherited scientific results at `dd8ee379d0d3518194c767d16402042525bc00dc`.

The repository is organised condition-first rather than protocol-first:

```text
C0  Can an interaction-dependent high-function state exist?
        ↓
C1  Does fragmentation / deterioration disrupt that state?
        ↓
C2  What distribution of functional-loss events does the eco-genetic system generate?
        ├─ recurrent state turnover
        ├─ genetic-state connectivity
        ├─ aggregate interaction support
        ├─ partner loss / explicit rewiring
        └─ process-resolved pollen movement
        ↓
    central loss incidence + among-block heterogeneity / tails
        ↓
    finite-panel calibration certificate (historical R4 rule)
        ↓
C3  Only after the loss process is characterised: does genetic change precede loss?
        ↓
C4  Is that warning portable across eco-genetic domains?
```

**Warning is a downstream conditional outcome, not the starting hypothesis.** If a general proposition is not recovered, the next result is its boundary or recovery condition.

## Current scientific state

### Inherited mechanism

The parent repository supplies the mechanistic base:

- interaction feedback can support a high-function state in the declared model;
- fragmenting the same prepared high state reduces interaction, local effective size and realised high-trait mass before population disappearance;
- in one independently calibrated symmetric benchmark, baseline-relative genetic-diversity erosion preceded observed functional loss, whereas fixed absolute thresholds produced both leads and lags.

Parent and extension trajectories are never pooled.

### Recovered extension results

1. **Source feasibility is conditional.** Across the original 15 recurrent-transition coordinates, 2,269/3,375 source attempts supported preparation/projection; support varied from 44.89% to 86.67%.
2. **Functional-loss outcomes are conditional.** Among 648 complete common-grid five-seed panels, 322 were rapid-loss, 242 persistence and 84 seed-heterogeneous. The original 15/15 `no_domain_selected` result remains immutable for that candidate family.
3. **Finite intermediate-risk validation panels can be recovered warning-blind.** Prospective high-rep work independently produced historical R4 certificates at `kappa_mu=.35, p_star=.35`. Phase J later shows why these certificates must not be promoted to sample-size-invariant biological regimes.
4. **Connectivity reshuffles loss histories without a universal rescue sign.** Phase E produced bidirectional paired loss-status switches. Its five-seed ensemble labelled `m=.10/.20` R3, but independent Phase-I/J ensembles show that the exact `m=.10` categorical boundary is not portable.
5. **Phase F is closed.** Aggregate interaction support `kappa=3.0,4.5,6.0` changed source eligibility (0.77, 0.94, 0.87) while all 15 tested seed-block rates remained in the operational intermediate-risk band; pooled loss was 0.468, 0.521 and 0.552. `interaction kappa` is **not partner richness**, connectance, pollinator diversity or network dimensionality.
6. **Partner loss changed reproducibility more than mean risk.** Phase G one-partner-loss conditions had pooled loss 0.556–0.578 versus intact 0.544, but broader block-to-block outcomes and finite R3 certificates. A paired incidence audit gave Cochran Q `p=.943`; contribution concentration itself did not separate the loss conditions.
7. **Network recovery did not guarantee functional-loss recovery.** Phase H rewiring increased active edges `3→5`, realised connectance `.500→.833` and final support multiplier `.750→.844`, while pooled loss changed only `.430→.419` and the downstream finite certificate remained R3.
8. **Pollen movement can be process-resolved.** Under the declared paternal-gene-flow closure, regional pollen fraction `g` is exactly equivalent to legacy global allele mixing `m=g/2`; Phase I verified `g=.20 ↔ m=.10` snapshot-exactly in 90/90 pairs. Regional versus ring pollen changed individual loss histories in both directions but shared the same finite certificate.
9. **The historical R4 all-block gate is ensemble-sensitive.** Phase J fixed the complete `m=.10` biological condition and obtained R4/R3/R4/R4 across four prospectively fixed five-seed panels. Across 20 fresh blocks, 19/20 rates were inside `[.30,.70]` and one was `.75`. Among all `C(20,5)=15,504` five-block combinations, 75% are R4 and 25% R3.
10. **Warning portability remains bounded.** Historical Protocol 003 compares separately calibrated, non-matched eco-genetic domains; its warning contrast is a portability result, not a single-factor transition-direction effect.

All finite numerical conclusions remain Type S evidence for the declared model closure.

## The key methodological correction

The biological object is now the **distribution of functional-loss events**, not a hard R4 label.

Primary upstream quantities are:

- central functional-loss incidence;
- among-block heterogeneity and tail behaviour;
- where useful, the fraction/probability of independent blocks falling inside an operational incidence zone, reported with uncertainty.

The historical R4 rule remains useful as a conservative **finite-panel calibration certificate**: all blocks in a particular validation panel must fall inside `[.30,.70]` before that panel is opened for warning analysis. But if a block passes with probability `q`, an independent `B`-block all-pass certificate passes with probability `q^B`. The binary certificate is therefore sampling-design dependent whenever `0<q<1`.

This distinction preserves the original condition-first logic while preventing a finite sampling rule from being mistaken for a biological phase boundary.

## Interaction and movement boundaries

### Aggregate interaction support

Phase F is closed. `interaction kappa` is aggregate positive-feedback/effective interaction support. It is **not partner richness**, connectance, pollinator diversity or network dimensionality, and no finer/wider kappa search is opened merely to manufacture a categorical boundary.

### Explicit partner/network state

Phase G is a reduced-form partner-loss closure. Phase H explicitly represents partner nodes, edge strengths, latent edges, trait matching, capacities and one fixed rewiring rule. Phase H does **not** show rewiring is generally ineffective; it shows that structural/connectance/support recovery is not sufficient evidence that the realised functional-loss distribution has recovered.

### Biological movement

Legacy `migration_rate` is allele-frequency mixing, not generic biological dispersal. Phase I identifies one narrow equivalence to a regional pollen closure. Seed/propagule movement, demographic recolonisation and partner movement remain distinct future biological operators rather than aliases for `migration_rate`.

## Urban and island translation

Urban and island systems are contrasting empirical routes through the condition space, **not ecological equivalents and not demonstrated here to occupy the same state**.

Because `functional fragmentation` already has an established landscape-ecology usage centred largely on organismal functional connectivity, the manuscript uses **interaction-mediated functional fragmentation** for the focal process here: loss or destabilisation of biotic interaction support required for realised ecological function while habitat or focal populations may remain present.

Measure separately:

- structural fragmentation, habitat amount and matrix quality;
- realised interaction support and partner identity;
- partner functional diversity, contribution evenness and rewiring;
- pollen, seed/propagule, demographic and partner movement;
- reproductive assurance / alternative functional routes;
- realised ecological function through time;
- among-population / among-year distribution of functional loss;
- genetic state through time.

The prospective cross-system question is:

> **Do different fragmentation mechanisms converge on similar interaction-mediated functional-loss distributions once state feasibility, realised function, process-resolved connectivity and among-unit heterogeneity are measured separately?**

Convergence does not require cities and islands to share geography, network topology, species composition, neutral genetic differentiation or a binary R4 certificate.

## Stop rules

- Do not inspect warning/diversity fields while selecting or characterising the upstream loss process.
- Do not tune `p_star`, connectivity, interaction kappa, partner weights, rewiring strength, pollen fraction, seed grouping or the historical R4 thresholds merely to obtain a desired categorical result.
- Do not overwrite a locked finite campaign because a later independent ensemble classifies differently.
- Do not call allele-frequency mixing generic demographic, pollen, seed or pollinator movement.
- Do not call interaction `kappa` network simplification.
- Do not call Phase H a universal rewiring theorem or infer functional recovery from connectance recovery alone.
- Do not call R4 a sample-size-invariant biological regime after Phase J.
- If generality fails or classification is ensemble-sensitive, record that boundary rather than tuning until the preferred label appears.
